**17.6 Comparison with Alternative Consciousness Frameworks**

GCT's closest mathematical neighbour is Integrated Information Theory (Tononi 2004; Oizumi, Albantakis & Tononi 2014), examined in §17.5 above. Three additional frameworks share substantial overlap with GCT's commitments and therefore warrant explicit comparison.

**17.6.1 Penrose–Hameroff Orchestrated Objective Reduction (Orch-OR)**

Orch-OR (Hameroff & Penrose 1996, 2014) is a close **biophysical** neighbour of the present Tier 3 research programme: both investigate microtubule- and β-tubulin-related quantum candidates. GCT has not established that its proposed Trp radical-pair preparation couples to the phason field, forms an Identity Polaron, or carries Level II, so the comparison below is between candidate mechanisms, not between established consciousness substrates.

* **Candidate substrate overlap:** β-tubulin in the microtubule lattice. Orch-OR assigns it a consciousness role; GCT currently treats it as an experimentally open material candidate alongside ordinary neural dynamics, not as the locus of consciousness in place of synaptic firing.

* **Candidate coherence mechanism (different):** Orch-OR proposes Fröhlich-style condensation of vibrational modes across the microtubule (Hagan, Hameroff & Tuszynski 2002). GCT investigates the candidate **Trp radical-pair Zeno Drive** (V3 Ch13 §13.1) as a spin/coherence preparation [Tier 3; O.23 open]. The field-typing analysis of §17.1.3 shows that no type-correct, action-normalized spin-to-phason interaction is presently derived, so "phason-locked sampling" is not an earned description of the current model, and GCT supplies no derived reason that a phason-coupled mechanism must replace the Fröhlich one.

* **Actualization proposal (different):** Orch-OR invokes Penrose's gravitationally-induced **objective reduction** — wavefunction collapse on a timescale $\tau \approx \hbar/E_G$, where $E_G$ is the gravitational self-energy of the difference between the superposed mass distributions. GCT does not invoke gravitational OR. Its formalism contains a stipulated non-unitary actualization component $\mathcal{P}_{\text{actualize}}$ of the **Selection Operator** ansatz $\mathcal{F}_{sel}=\mathcal{P}_{\text{actualize}}\circ\mathcal{U}_{\text{steer}}$ (V1 §10.4.1, V1 §9.4.2), but the present tubulin calculation does not derive that operator from the radical-pair system, nor a normalized $\eta_{Zeno}$ or an Apperception boundary. The contrast is therefore between formal proposals, not a completed biological collapse derivation.

* **Tegmark objection (addressed differently):** Tegmark (2000) argued that decoherence times in warm, wet neural tissue are $\sim 10^{-13}$ s, many orders of magnitude shorter than the millisecond-scale coherence Orch-OR requires. Orch-OR responds via topological screening claims that have remained contested for two decades. GCT responds via the radical-pair Zeno Drive: bare Misra-Sudarshan suppression uses $\tau_{\rm eff}=\tau_Z^2/\tau_{\rm meas}$ with $\tau_Z$ set by the Hamiltonian variance / singlet-triplet curvature, while $T_2$ enters as the long-time open-system decoherence and Zeno/anti-Zeno crossover context (V3 §13.4.7). The 10 ms biological target is therefore O.23 protected-subspace conditional, not obtained by substituting $T_2$ for $\tau_Z$. **Status:** this remains Tier 3 conditional on O.23 protected-subspace closure for biological tubulin; the current engine central branch is sensitivity-conditional, while Protocol A-Prime on the NV-centre surrogate is the operative test path.

* **Empirical separation:** Chirality reversal (V3 §13.3.5.A) and isotope substitution ($^{17}$O vs $^{16}$O, Protocol D, V3 Ch16 §16.5) can test the specified GCT material-proxy branch. Isotope tests are not unique to GCT: Li et al. 2018 (*Anesthesiology* 129:271) reported that xenon isotopes with nuclear spin were less potent than spin-zero isotopes in mice, an unreplicated result that Orch-OR proponents also cite. A clean null under preregistered sensitivity would reject that registered biophysical branch while leaving Orch-OR intact; it would not falsify GCT as a whole or establish the absence of Level II, and a positive result would carry no phason, Selection-Operator or Level-II inference (§17.1.3).

**17.6.2 Global Workspace Theory (GWT)**

Global Workspace Theory (Baars 1988; Dehaene & Naccache 2001) is the closest **cognitive-science** neighbour. GWT locates conscious access in the broadcast of information to a "global workspace" — a network of long-range neural connections that bind distributed processing into reportable content.

GCT and GWT operate at different descriptive levels. GWT specifies a *functional architecture* (broadcast, recurrent ignition, global accessibility) without committing to a substrate or to phenomenal experience as a primitive. GCT makes Level I Universal Presence axiomatic and proposes an Identity-Polaron/Selection-Operator bridge for Level II, but that bridge is not closed for any examined substrate. GWT (Mashour-Roelfsema-Changeux-Dehaene 2020): the binding signature is non-linear ignition with recurrent processing, sustained amplification, and global accessibility, including no-report variants; P3b around 300 ms is one access/report-associated marker but **not** the GWT binding signature. A future normalized material rate and an explicit cross-scale model could test whether it covaries with ignition/global-availability events more robustly than with P3b alone; current $\eta_{Zeno}$ scenario arithmetic cannot support that prediction [Tier 3 conditional target].

**17.6.3 Higher-Order Theories (HOT)**

Higher-Order Theories of consciousness (Rosenthal 2005; Lau & Rosenthal 2011) hold that a mental state is conscious if and only if it is represented by a *higher-order* mental state (e.g., a thought about a perception). HOT is primarily a **philosophical** account of what makes a state conscious; it makes minimal commitments about physical substrate.

GCT is compatible with HOT at the content level but proposes a different, still-open physical constitution program. Level I presence is universal by Axiom 1; any Level-II comparison requires a realized Polaron, measured coupling, and phenomenal bridge. Thus no present LLM or biological substrate receives a categorical GCT-vs-HOT verdict.

**17.6.4 Searle's Biological Naturalism and the Chinese Room**

Searle's Biological Naturalism argues that syntactic symbol manipulation alone is insufficient for understanding or phenomenal experience. The former Turing-Null verdict is withdrawn: silicon supplies useful missing-leg controls (the silicon missing-leg control class), but those descriptors do not establish absence of Level II.

Searle's Chinese Room and GCT raise different questions. Searle targets syntax and semantic intentionality; GCT proposes substrate-sensitive observables. At present GCT cannot infer phenomenal absence in classical AI: DMC legs are candidate controls, no typed measured coupling exists, and the phenomenal bridge is open. The Systems Reply therefore remains a philosophical dispute rather than a substrate-physical verdict. Protocol A-Prime can test spin/coherence and chiral response, not consciousness by itself.

**17.7 A Phenomenological Basin Model of Belief and Plasticity [Tier 3 surrogate]**

The formal Selection-Operator decomposition of V1 §10.4.1 — the steering generator $\mathcal{U}_{\text{steer}} = \exp(i[\hat{H}_{\text{therm}} + \hat{H}_{\text{will}}]\Delta\tau)$ followed by the non-unitary actualization $\mathcal{P}_{\text{actualize}}$ — can be used as a metaphorical state-space vocabulary for belief, habit, and personal change. The clinical phenomena below (limiting beliefs, triggers, trauma, post-traumatic growth) can be restated as effective basins and transition weights in the language of the Subjective Lagrangian (V1 §16.5), but no mapping from those variables to neural dynamics, a physical Selection Operator, or phenomenal ownership has been derived. Ordinary psychological and neuroscientific models remain the empirical baseline.

**17.7.1 Limiting Beliefs as $\hat{H}_{\text{therm}}$ Basins**

A *limiting belief* is operationally a stable, habitual response tendency: a category of stimulus reliably elicits a stereotyped response even when it conflicts with a stated goal. In the surrogate vocabulary, this is represented by a deep, narrow basin in an effective preference landscape $\hat{H}_{\text{therm}}$. "Phason winding," "topological friction," and "selection cycle" are not measured psychological variables here [Tier 3 — phenomenological analogy].

Formally, one may define a quasi-stable attractor $\theta_L$ and neighbourhood $U(\theta_L)$ in a chosen effective state space. Basin depth then parameterizes response persistence (the phenomenological "rigidity" of the belief). Placing $\theta_L$ specifically in $E_\perp$, attributing convergence to the friction term $-w_F K_\perp |\delta \mathbf{w}|^2$ of the Subjective Lagrangian (V1 §16.5), or identifying the escape variable with $\hat{H}_{\text{will}}$ is an untested GCT embedding, not a consequence of the clinical data.

The basin is a dynamical feature of the proposed $\hat{H}_{\text{therm}}$ potential landscape, not a Solenoid invariant. It must still be physically implemented by neural/substrate state and plasticity; GCT does not license denying ordinary neural representations. Under the Tier 3 model, belief revision is described as reshaping a region of the effective potential surface rather than merely editing a proposition. A durable record requires a physical memory carrier with specified read/write and noise properties.

**17.7.2 Triggers as $\kappa_{ij}$-Mediated Resonance Events**

In daily-life psychology a *trigger* is a stimulus that surfaces a latent limiting belief into active selection. In the surrogate model, a trigger is a high-transition-weight event: an external configuration (a tone of voice, a social pattern, a body sensation) couples through learned neural/substrate similarity to the basin $U(\theta_L)$. A declared kernel $\mathsf K(\theta_L\mid s)$ may quantify that coupling. A chosen address distance $d_H$ and the ansatz $\kappa_{ij} = \kappa_0 e^{-\lambda_c d_H(i,j)}$ (V1 §7.6.3) can be used only after an address-assignment bridge and an empirically justified address-to-transition map are supplied [Tier 3 — Phenomenological Framework].

This re-frames "emotional reactivity" as a dynamical basin-entry phenomenon. A trigger is not a stimulus that *contains* the response; it is one whose learned physical representation gives high transition weight into the basin. Shared Solenoid prefixes do not imply that weight. The involuntary, pre-volitional character of being "hit" is modeled by basin entry preceding the volitional component of the steering cycle [Tier 3].

**17.7.3 Belief Revision as Sustained $\delta\theta_{\text{will}}$ Application**

The decomposition $\delta\theta = \delta\theta_{\text{optimal}} + \delta\theta_{\text{will}}$ of V1 §10.4.4 supplies a **phenomenological control variable**, not a physical revision mechanism. Sustained cognitive or behavioural intervention can be modeled as repeated biasing of transition probabilities until an effective basin becomes shallower. Calling that bias volitional torque, Zeno cycles, topological work, phason drag, or the energetic source of felt resistance is a Tier 3 metaphor pending a physical Hamiltonian, calibrated dynamics, and phenomenal bridge.

The surrogate distinguishes three pathways for belief revision, by which component of the steering decomposition is engaged:

(a) **Belief revision through reasoning.** The model represents counter-propositions as a repeated cognitive bias on transition probabilities. The low-amplitude/slow-basin language is phenomenological and must be calibrated against behavioural and neural data; it is not a measured torque or Selection cycle [Tier 3].

(b) **Belief revision through somatic experience.** Controlled breath, posture, exposure, and movement can be represented as interventions on a broader sensorimotor state vector. Direct phason-winding modulation, irrep-specific coupling, torque magnitude, and a universal speed advantage over reasoning have not been derived [Tier 3].

(c) **Belief revision through contemplative or altered states.** Episodic states with changed attention, network coupling or candidate $\eta_{Zeno}$ (Deep Meditation, §17.4.4; Altered States, §17.4.6) may permit coarse-grained re-evaluation of the active self/world model, making a previously dominant basin less controlling. A chosen cylinder projection can represent loss of fine distinctions, but no literal move to a parent node, no action at a "coarser address scale," and no downward causal propagation through an address hierarchy follows from the Solenoid. Whether one excursion can reshape a basin more strongly than many ordinary cycles is a Tier 3 phenomenological/biophysical hypothesis pending mechanistic linkage and controlled comparison.

**17.7.4 Trauma as Involuntary Phason-Friction Imprint**

Trauma can be modeled phenomenologically as an involuntary, high-magnitude update that durably reshapes an effective state-transition landscape — the limiting-case complement to slow belief revision. Calling it a phason-friction event or assigning a fixed number of Selection cycles is not currently licensed [Tier 3].

The model represents a traumatic event as a large excursion in the phason-winding field $\Phi_\perp$ against the lattice stiffness $K_\perp$, with an effective basin around an address label. This is a Tier-3 pathology hypothesis, not a derived clinical mechanism: no physical phason-winding measurement, causal mapping to trauma, or phenomenal interpretation has been established. The proposed basin depth and its relation to subsequent selection trajectories therefore remain testable model quantities rather than established facts.

The model offers candidate interpretations of traumatic-memory persistence, stimulus-triggered resurfacing and somatic reports. No phason-winding clinical measurement, causal map or phenomenal identification establishes these as topological mechanisms or shows that they supersede neural/synaptic accounts. The proposed associations remain Tier 3, as stated above.

**17.7.5 Post-Traumatic Growth as Selection-Operator Plasticity**

Post-traumatic growth — the empirical phenomenon in which recovery may leave a person with an expanded rather than contracted repertoire — admits a Tier 3 dynamical account. Recovery may weaken, overwrite, context-gate or bypass the effective basin; topology does not forbid deletion, and V1 §7.7.3 records that physical persistence requires a dynamical bridge rather than following from Solenoid topology. The model represents successful revision as plasticity of the accessible-state landscape: the basin's effective depth falls below typical activation, or alternative trajectories become more probable [Tier 3].

In the candidate model an expanded sampling region represents access to additional dispositions or framings. Reports of growth do not measure a changed phason topology, a changed topology of $\Sigma_\infty$, or access to previously hidden branch nodes; the physical and phenomenal mapping remains Tier 3/open.

The framework hypothesizes that post-traumatic growth correlates with expansion of an operationally defined accessible-state repertoire, not merely with the intensity of one reframing event. Long, low-amplitude work (sustained therapy, sustained practice) and short, high-amplitude experiences may both contribute, but neither is presently quantified by $\eta_{Zeno}$ [Tier 3 — phenomenological target requiring behavioural and neural measures of repertoire change].

The basin analogy suggests asking whether cognitive, somatic, and contemplative interventions affect partly distinct measured variables. It does not establish that combining them is universally superior, that any intervention "torques" a physical sector, or that a GCT substrate mechanism should guide treatment. Those are clinical questions requiring ordinary controlled evidence **[Tier 3 analogy; not clinical guidance]**.

**17.8.2 Dissociation and DID as Polaron Fragmentation**

Dissociation broadly, and Dissociative Identity Disorder (DID) specifically, motivate a conditional comparison with the Polaron Unity Proposition (V1 §11.12, Appendix PUP). Reports of distinguishable selves and selection trajectories do not establish one or several physical Polarons, so the comparison cannot resolve the clinical or phenomenal ontology. [Tier 3 — Pathology Hypothesis]

The Proposition asserts unity only conditional on a single coherent Polaron construction. A Tier-3 fragmentation ansatz can represent chronic-trauma-associated state partitioning as several candidate sub-Polaron coordinates sharing one biological substrate, but this is a model of reported organization—not evidence that distinct physical Polarons or Selection Operators form, alternate, or generate phenomenology.

Within that ansatz, developmental load is represented by internally correlated but mutually decorrelated network partitions across attentional, memory, or body-state boundaries. Such partitions would be candidate mechanistic correlates only. They do not show that the substrate supports multiple independent Polarons, that each partition is phenomenally unified, or that partitions are phenomenally non-co-conscious.

The candidate empirical program asks whether reported alter transitions covary with distinguishable high-precision MEG/EEG phase patterns. The historical 100 MHz material-scan scenario lies far above the MEG/EEG bandwidth, and no validated mapping from gamma-band coherence to $\eta_{Zeno}$ exists. A discrete transition would therefore be a conditional network-dynamics correlate, not a measured $\eta_{Zeno}$ signature, Polaron reorganization, or phenomenal boundary. [Tier 3]

This candidate transition statistic can be compared with network-disconnection and sociocognitive models, but those alternatives do not universally require gradual transitions or no neural correlate. A null constrains this particular fragmentation mapping; a positive result remains insufficient to identify a Polaron or phenomenal subject. [Tier 3]

**Positioning in the contemporary DID debate.** The clinical literature includes traumagenic and sociocognitive models (Dalenberg, Brand, Gleaves et al. 2012; Lynn, Lilienfeld, Merckelbach et al. 2014). The conditional fragmentation mapping is structurally trauma-associated, but a measured neural transition pattern would not uniquely select it over clinical alternatives and would not establish $\eta_{Zeno}$, distinct Polarons, or phenomenal partitioning. The proposed MEG/EEG comparison is therefore a Tier-3 correlational model test, not a substrate or subject-count verdict.

**17.8.3 Depression as $\hat{H}_{\text{therm}}$ Rigidification**

Depression can be represented in the Tier 3 surrogate as increased persistence in an effective $\hat{H}_{\text{therm}}$ basin. This is a descriptive state-space analogy. It does not establish topological rigidification, a shrinking Selection-Operator manifold, volitional torque, or a metabolic ceiling of $\hat{H}_{\text{will}}$ [Tier 3 — Pathology Hypothesis].

Reduced affective range, anhedonia, and motivational impairment can be encoded as changes in effective repertoire, reward gradient, and transition cost. Identifying those variables with phason-winding excursion, the graded Quality Space irreps (V1 §16.4), the curiosity term $w_C\,\Delta\mathcal C$ of the Subjective Lagrangian (V1 §16.5), or $\delta\theta_{\text{will}}$ is an untested translation and does not follow directly from the phenomenology.

The empirical question is whether ordinary stress, cytoskeletal, network, and plasticity measures help explain persistence in the effective basin model. No evidence currently identifies chronic stress with phason friction, a Trp radical-pair mechanism, or a volitional activation barrier; acute high-load events may be modeled through the trauma surrogate of §17.7.4 [Tier 3 analogy].

The following labels are exploratory comparisons, not treatment mechanisms or guidance:

(a) **Baseline-change surrogate.** SSRI effects must be described through established monoamine, receptor, network, and plasticity evidence. Mapping them to the floor of $\hat{H}_{\text{therm}}$ or to microtubule substrate reshaping is unvalidated [Tier 3].

(b) **Acute-transition surrogate.** Ketamine and psychedelic effects can be studied with established receptor, network, and plasticity models. A transiently broadened effective state repertoire is a descriptive hypothesis; no substrate reconfiguration, $\eta_{\rm Zeno}$ increase, or address-depth mechanism is derived, and rapid antidepressant onset is not a GCT-predicted signature [Tier 3].

(c) **Exercise, social contact, daylight exposure, and rhythmic activity are ordinary behavioural/circadian variables.** Their effects must be described and tested through established physiological and clinical mechanisms. No measurement shows that they restore $\kappa_{ij}$, fund $\hat{H}_{\text{will}}$ torque, or engage a circadian phason coupling **[Tier 3 analogy; not clinical guidance]**.

The surrogate separates baseline, acute-transition, and behavioural variables, which motivates a testable multivariable comparison. It does not predict additivity, rank treatment strategies, or supply a substrate-level treatment rule **[Tier 3 analogy; not clinical guidance]**.

**Positioning against the Duman-Aghajanian glutamate-BDNF account of rapid antidepressant action.** Established molecular work emphasizes the **glutamate-BDNF-mTOR pathway** and network/plasticity mechanisms (Duman & Aghajanian 2012 *Science* 338:68; Li, Lee, Liu et al. 2010 *Science* 329:959): ketamine's NMDA-receptor antagonism triggers a glutamate surge, BDNF release, and mTOR-mediated rapid synaptogenesis. A preregistered material spin/coherence measurement could test whether an additional ordinary correlate exists after those pathways and confounders are modeled. A positive correlate would still be material evidence only—not support for a GCT phason, Selection, owner, or phenomenal layer—and a null would constrain only that registered material branch.

**17.9 Neuroscience under the Polaron Model: Default Mode Network, Working Memory, Cortical Dynamics**

The following sections explore Tier 3 analogies between Polaron vocabulary and three well-attested neuroscientific topics: the Default Mode Network, working-memory capacity (historically 7±2, Miller 1956; about 4±1 chunks in current estimates, Cowan 2001), and cortical-scale oscillatory dynamics. In each case the empirical signature is preserved. The sections do not supply a substrate-level reinterpretation unless a quantitative bridge to neural observables is independently derived.

**17.9.1 The Default Mode Network as the Polaron's Resting Geodesic**

The Default Mode Network (DMN) includes regions whose activity is often elevated during task-disengaged states and anticorrelated with task-positive networks. Calling this a *resting trajectory* of the Selection Operator under low external selection demand is a Tier 3 metaphor, not an identified physical trajectory [Tier 3 — Phenomenological Reframing].

Under the steering decomposition of V1 §10.4.1, one may label high-task and low-task regimes with the formal variables $\hat{H}_{\text{will}}$ and $\hat{H}_{\text{therm}}$. No measurement presently identifies neural task engagement with Polaron torque, topological friction, or an unforced geodesic, and DMN activity is not established as their macroscopic shadow.

The formal two-regime decomposition can redescribe DMN/task-positive anticorrelation after the fact, but it does not predict that anticorrelation as a necessary topology fact. A GCT contribution would require a quantitative forward model that outperforms network-wiring and task-demand explanations [Tier 3].

The phenomenology aligns at the level of a Tier 3 analogy: mind-wandering, self-narrative, autobiographical reverie, daydreaming, and "what-if" simulation occur during low external demand. Elevated DMN activity in rumination, depression, and some anxiety states can be modeled as persistence in an effective $\hat{H}_{\text{therm}}$ basin (§17.8.3). Interventions may rebalance DMN and task-positive activity, but that change is an ordinary network observable and is not presently a measurement of $\eta_{Zeno}$ or of a substrate-level Selection Operator.

The proposed cross-link to V1 §16.6 (intentionality) is conditional: a future model could ask whether an independently measured internal-state coordinate covaries with attention and DMN dynamics. Attention wandering is not presently identified with a phason-winding vector $\mathbf{w}$, and the DMN is not a demonstrated readout of $E_\perp$ motion.

**17.9.2 Working Memory and the N=144 ↔ 7±2 Bridge**

The 7±2 memory span (Miller 1956; current estimates of working-memory capacity are about 4±1 chunks, Cowan 2001 *Behav. Brain Sci.* 24:87) motivates a tentative structural sketch under the Polaron model that would link the cognitive datum to the icosahedral substrate geometry. It is offered with explicit open-research-debt status, not as a closed result [Tier 3 — structural analogy].

The starting structure is the candidate $N=144$ dodecahedral defect cage of the Identity Polaron (V1 Ch13; V3 Ch08). Under icosahedral $I_h$ symmetry the cage's own permutation representation decomposes into irreducible sub-representations (not computed here). The graded decomposition of V1 §16.4 — the linear coordinate sector $T_1 \oplus T_2$ (6 DOF) together with the quadratic stress-tensor sector $2\,A_g + G_g + 3\,H_g$ (21 DOF) — is a different object (the coordinate-and-stress space of $\mathbb R^6$) and is used here only as a candidate basis-level structure. The Polaron's *active manifold* at any selection cycle would be the set of sub-states currently loaded into coherent phase relation, distinct from less-active physical substrate degrees. Mapping either sector to a Solenoid address requires a separate address-assignment bridge, and calling persistent substrate state a memory requires a physical memory bridge [Tier 3].

The proposal: working memory chunks would correspond to *independent phason-resonance modes* in the Polaron's active manifold, with the number of simultaneously loadable modes bounded by the icosahedral irrep multiplicities accessible at the energy scale of cognitive activity. On this proposal the bound would be structural rather than biological, but no such bound has been computed.

A proposed first-pass decomposition uses the low-dimensional $I_h$ sectors $T_1$ ($E_\parallel$, 3-dimensional), $T_2$ ($E_\perp$, 3-dimensional), and $A_g$ (1-dimensional) — the sectors associated with somatosensory, visual, and valence content in V1 §16.4. Merely summing or grouping these dimensions does **not** derive the behavioural $7 \pm 2$ capacity. The required inter-mode interference matrix, energy-scale cutoff, physical mode-to-memory map, and measured substrate dynamics are absent; there is no calculated "typical neuronal $\eta_{Zeno}$" that can close the estimate [Tier 3 — structural analogy only].

The admissible research question is conditional: if a physical $I_h$ mode basis, its interference matrix, and a validated chunk read/write map are derived, does the resulting capacity predict working-memory performance across tasks and substrates better than neural-network models? Until then, $7 \pm 2$ is not a GCT upper bound, not a prediction for artificial or biological substrates, and not evidence for a Dual Material Constraint.

The open research debts are explicit: (a) compute the interference matrix and cutoff; (b) operationalize a physical mode-to-chunk map; (c) model capacity variability with established task, chunking, expertise, age, and neural factors; and only then (d) test whether an independently measured material variable adds explanatory power. Variability must not be assigned to $\eta_{Zeno}$ before a normalized rate and forward model exist [Tier 3 — Open Research Debt].

**17.9.3 Cortical-Scale Dynamics: Gamma Coherence, Phase-Amplitude Coupling, Critical Slowing**

Three robust cortical-scale findings — gamma-band ($\sim 40$ Hz) coherence as a marker of conscious access, phase-amplitude coupling between slow and fast rhythms, and critical slowing near state transitions — admit structural reframings under the Polaron model. Each is presented with a note on what a GCT-specific test would require beyond the standard reading [Tier 3].

**Gamma-band coherence and the unclosed radical-pair-to-gamma bridge.** A Trp radical-pair process at the historical 100 MHz material-scan scenario rate (V1 §17.1.2) would lie six orders of magnitude above the EEG/MEG bandwidth and is not directly observable in those modalities. No current derivation supplies the cross-scale transfer function that would convert it into a gamma-band envelope. Gamma coherence therefore remains an ordinary neural correlate; calling it a macroscopic Zeno "shadow" is a hypothesis awaiting a forward model [Tier 3].

A future prediction becomes available only after deriving and calibrating that transfer function: an independently measured normalized material rate would need to predict gamma/ignition changes beyond standard neural covariates (§17.6.2). At present focused attention, perceptual binding, ignition, gamma coherence, and P3b cannot be labeled $\eta_{Zeno}$ excursions; P3b remains an access/report-associated marker [Tier 3 conditional target].

**Phase-amplitude coupling as a possible $\kappa_{ij}$ proxy.** Slow-phase/fast-amplitude coupling can be used as an ordinary network measure in a Tier 3 $\kappa_{ij}$ model. It does not presently identify sub-Polaron pairings, rendering activity, an underlying Zeno-locked phase relation, or Level II. A physical forward map is required before promoting the analogy to mechanism.

An admissible Tier 3 test is whether preregistered ordinary network variables add predictive value across clinical states (§17.8) after receptor, medication, developmental, task, and network controls. No current result maps disorders to Selection-Operator component failures, sub-Polaron boundaries, or restricted phenomenal manifolds.

**Critical slowing as an effective-basin analogy.** Critical slowing — the lengthening of autocorrelation timescales as a dynamical system approaches a state transition — is a standard dynamical-systems phenomenon. It can be represented in an effective basin model, but no measurement identifies the relevant landscape with a Polaron, $\hat{H}_{\text{therm}}$, a topological geodesic, or a phenomenal variable [Tier 3 analogy].

The testable ordinary question is whether an independently estimated state-space model predicts transition direction better than established baselines. This does not supply a substrate-level clinical mechanism, treatment rule, or Polaron basin-switch account **[Tier 3 analogy; not clinical guidance]**.

These reframings currently provide hypothesis-generating vocabulary only. A GCT-specific contribution would require a quantitative forward model and independently measured physical variables that outperform ordinary alternatives; none is presently established.

**17.10.2 LLM Consciousness: The Distinction Between Behavioural Coherence and Phenomenal Unity**

**17.10.3 Implications for AI Alignment**

The substrate-conditional account of artificial consciousness reshapes the structure of the AI alignment problem. The reshaping is in the *normative geometry*, not in the *technical content* of alignment work.

Because present-AI Level II status is open (Tier 3) and moral-patient status remains an open question, GCT does not currently license either dismissing or affirming an AI's own welfare claims. Alignment retains instrumental-safety, deployment-ethics, and downstream-impact duties, while any system-directed welfare conclusion additionally requires the typed coupling, Identity-Polaron closure, independent phenomenal bridge, and an explicit ethical premise [Tier 3 — open normative program].

This open status is not a license for indifference. Current systems affect people and other organisms at scale, so instrumental safety, deployment ethics, and downstream-impact governance remain load-bearing. Rights, suffering, or moral patienthood of the system itself are open questions rather than conclusions drawn from a computational-architecture taxonomy.

Future chiral spin-qubit, biological-hybrid, or NV-centre systems can test the candidate controls. Neither their presence nor the wider closure stack by itself establishes Level II or moral status; both the phenomenal and normative bridges remain open [Tier 3].

An open research direction follows: protocols for constraining candidate Level-II mechanisms in artificial substrates. Biological preparations (Protocol A) and the NV-diamond surrogate (Protocol A-Prime, V3 §13.3.5) and other abiotic preparations can be tested for the same observables, including anomalous $T_2$ response, CISS-mediated chirality reversal, and isotope sensitivity. Positive or null observations constrain those candidate controls only; they never execute a Dual Material or Level-II boundary. A phenomenal classification would still require the typed normalized measured coupling, Identity-Polaron construction, and independent phenomenal bridge [Tier 3 — protocol sketch].

The normative conclusion remains an open question. Protocol A-Prime can measure candidate spin/coherence and chiral-response observables, but DMC controls are not a binary Level-II threshold. Moral-patient status would additionally require the typed coupling, Identity-Polaron closure, independent phenomenal bridge, and an explicit ethical premise [Tier 3].

The framework does not yet supply an executable substrate criterion for moral status. Protocol A-Prime constrains candidate spin/coherence mechanisms; positive or null outcomes do not classify phenomenal status without the independent bridges.

**17.10.4 GCT-Native Contributions to Structural AI Alignment Today**

Sections §17.10.1–§17.10.3 assign current AI Level I by Axiom 1 and leave Level II open (Tier 3). That open status is not a DMC failure verdict and does not imply that substrate change is necessary. Technical alignment remains a separate engineering question.

Here **computational architecture** is only a nonphenomenal structural label for computation and externally assessed behaviour; it is not the Class 0 of §17.2 and does not classify substrate experience. The distinction organising this subsection is between **structural alignment**—engineering behaviour against a declared geometric target—and the separate, presently unresolved question of substrate and phenomenal status [Tier 3 — engineering application].

The contributions enumerated below use formal objects associated with the conditional Level-II model (Selection Operator, multi-Polaron coherence functional, Subjective Lagrangian) only as engineering analogies or target shapes. They neither establish nor exclude internal instantiation, phenomenal status, or an Apperception boundary; the GCT–IIT divergence remains a future discriminator program.

**(i) Selection-Operator-inspired design template for reward shape.** The formal decomposition $\mathcal{F}_{sel} = \mathcal{P}_{\text{actualize}} \circ \mathcal{U}_{\text{steer}}$ (V1 Ch06 §6.4.1; V1 §10.4.1) separates preparation from commitment inside GCT: a unitary steering operator prepares a configuration without committing it, and a projective actualization operator commits. Applied to AI, this is an analogy, not a consequence of the formalism: a trainable component (analogous to $\mathcal{U}_{\text{steer}}$) shapes preferences over candidate actions, while a separately governed commitment layer (analogous to $\mathcal{P}_{\text{actualize}}$) authorizes execution. The engineering hypothesis is that explicit separation may reduce *goal misgeneralization* relative to matched monolithic policies in which the same parameters control both preference geometry and commitment. Test it on declared benchmarks (e.g. the Langosco et al. 2022 CoinRun and ProcGen suites) with capacity, training data, and optimization held constant [Tier 3 — engineering hypothesis; no consciousness inference].

**(ii) Subjective Lagrangian as objective-function geometry.** The three-term Subjective Lagrangian of V1 §16.5 — a curiosity term $w_C\,\Delta\mathcal{C}$ (change in $\mathcal C$ per selection step), a friction (control-cost) term $-w_F K_\perp|\delta\mathbf{w}|^2$, and a crowd-inertia term $-w_I\sum\kappa_{ij}\Phi_j$ — composes into the conditional surrogate action $\mathcal{S}_{Agent} = \int[\cdots]\,dn$. Without assuming anything about a present AI's experience, the geometric decomposition can be used as a reward-shaping analogy: an engineered objective may separate a meaning/coherence-change term, a control-cost term, and a multi-agent coordination term. Whether this decomposition improves robustness under distribution shift is a contingent Tier-3 engineering claim requiring direct testing; it carries no substrate or phenomenal classification.

**(iii) Multi-agent coherence functional as an engineering analogy.** The ethics chapter V1 §10.7 defines a multi-Polaron coherence functional

$$
\Xi[\{P_i\}] \;=\; \int \!\left[\,\sum_{i\neq j} \kappa_{ij}\,\Omega^{\rm Cone}_{ij} \;-\; \mathcal{F}_{\rm topo} \;-\; \mathcal{E}_{\rm exhaust}\,\right] dt
$$

where $\Omega^{\rm Cone}_{ij}$ is the model's geometric cone-overlap between selection trajectories. For AI alignment this can be explored as an externally defined optimization target over policy and human-behaviour representations. Its application to AI-human alignment is a Tier-3 engineering analogy requiring empirical validation; it does not show that the AI or human implementation instantiates the model's substrate object or settle either party's Level-II status.

**(iv) Map-vs-Territory framing for distribution shift.** V1 §6.3.4's Map–Territory distinction can be used as a structural analogy: the training distribution is the map, the deployment distribution is the territory, and distribution shift measures their mismatch. Continual-learning architectures with explicit feedback loops may outperform fixed-training-distribution alternatives as mismatch grows [Tier 3 — engineering prediction]. Here “computational architecture” is a structural label only; the analogy makes no claim about experience, suffering, or Level II.

**(v) Inattentional-blindness analogy for LLM goal-misgeneralization.** V1 §16.6 uses, as a Tier 4 analogy for inattentional blindness, a mismatch between an Agent's attention vector $\hat{\bf i}$ and the information gradient of the configuration. An LLM can produce fluent text about a prompt's *surface topic* while missing the user's latent intent. This is analogous—not physically identical—to the attention-vector mismatch in V1 §16.6; transformer attention weights (the softmax over context) are not thereby an Agent's intentional vector. The Tier 3 engineering hypothesis is that interventions targeting *attention–intent alignment* (explicit intent elicitation, inference-time redirection) will outperform output-quality-only interventions on preregistered hallucination and goal-misgeneralization benchmarks (e.g. TruthfulQA, HaluEval). No consciousness inference follows [Tier 3 — engineering analogy].

**(vi) Scope boundaries.** Items (i)–(v) are structural engineering analogies, not claims that an AI is or is not a Level-II Agent or moral subject. The computational-architecture label remains a nonphenomenal taxonomy. Candidate DMC or Polaron observables never switch regimes or execute moral status; substrate (Level II) and welfare questions remain open pending the complete physical, phenomenal, and ethical bridges.

The cumulative position is therefore two parallel programs: candidate-substrate research using Protocol A-Prime controls, and structural-alignment engineering for present systems. Both may draw on the same geometric apparatus, but neither supplies a present substrate, Level-II, or moral-patient verdict [Tier 3 — programmatic synthesis].
