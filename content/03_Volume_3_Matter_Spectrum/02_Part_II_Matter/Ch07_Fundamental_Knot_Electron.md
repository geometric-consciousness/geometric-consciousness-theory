 We model the electron defect as a discrete topological sphere ($S^2$) embedded in the projected 5-fold lattice. The **Gauss-Bonnet Theorem** for a discrete surface states:
 $$ \sum_{v \in \text{Defect}} \kappa_v = 2\pi \chi(S^2) = 4\pi $$

 $$\kappa_I=2\pi-5\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right)=\frac{\pi}{3},\qquad 12\kappa_I=4\pi.$$

 $$\kappa_D=2\pi-3\left(\frac{3\pi}{5}\right)=\frac{\pi}{5},\qquad 20\kappa_D=4\pi.$$
 Thus both polyhedra satisfy discrete Gauss--Bonnet exactly. The theorem confirms the closed spherical boundary geometry; it does **not** determine 12 sub-nodes per dodecahedral face and therefore does not derive $N=144$.

The regular icosahedron has defect $\pi/3$ at each of 12 vertices, and the regular dodecahedron has defect $\pi/5$ at each of 20 vertices; both totals equal $4\pi$. $$N_{\text{cage}}=12_{\text{outer faces}}\times12_{\text{inner sub-nodes}}=144.$$
