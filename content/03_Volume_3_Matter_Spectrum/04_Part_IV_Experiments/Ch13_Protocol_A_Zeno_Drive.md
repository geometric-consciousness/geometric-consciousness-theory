> **Joint-gate sample-size calculation.** For a normalized single-shot contrast estimate with Gaussian shot noise, a one-component $3\sigma$ gate with 80% marginal power uses
> $$N_{\rm shots}\geq \left(\frac{3+0.84}{\delta}\right)^2,$$
> where $\delta$ is the fractional effect size after the S0-S7 systematic budget. The conservative pre-registered effect sizes are $\delta_{T_2}=0.10$ for the non-monotonic selected-branch peak, $\delta_{\chi}=0.05$ for the chirality contrast after cap-purity, transfer retention, and NV-depth dilution, and $\delta_{150}=0.10$ for powering the sign-unfixed 150 MHz diagnostic response. Therefore:
> $$N_{T_2}\geq 1.48\times10^3,\qquad N_{\chi}\geq 5.90\times10^3,\qquad N_{150}\geq 1.48\times10^3.$$
> These design effect sizes ($\delta_{T_2}$, $\delta_{\chi}$, $\delta_{150}$) are locked by the blinded pilot calibration before unblinding the decisive run. The $\delta_{150}$ and $N_{150}$ entries power the sign-unfixed diagnostic only and never determine F1 or a Holm primary decision. If the pilot lower edge falls below the registered branch-feature or chirality values, that F1 component is sensitivity-limited and must be re-powered rather than treated as a clean F1 proxy rejection. The conservative acquisition design retains 93% marginal power per measured component ($z_\beta \approx 1.48$), giving $N_{T_2}\geq 2.01\times10^3$, $N_{\chi}\geq 8.03\times10^3$, and diagnostic $N_{150}\geq 2.01\times10^3$ per relevant fixed-frequency cell. > $$N_{T_2,\rm scan}^{\rm raw}=5012.64,\qquad N_{T_2,\rm scan}^{\rm executable}=5013$$

### §13.3.6 Protocol A-Tertius: Leggett-Garg Test of the Material Coherence Proxy

**Objective (historical).** Protocol A-Tertius proposed a Leggett-Garg Inequality (LGI) test of temporal coherence in a radical-pair or NV material system driven in the registered branch windows. It is now a declared open programme (below); no registered gate remains.

**The Leggett-Garg Framework.** The Leggett-Garg Inequalities (Leggett & Garg, 1985) constrain the temporal correlations of a macroscopic two-state system under the assumptions of **macrorealism** (the system is in a definite state at all times) and **non-invasive measurability** (measurement does not disturb the state). For a dichotomic observable $Q(t) \in \{+1, -1\}$, the temporal correlation functions $C_{ij} = \langle Q(t_i) Q(t_j) \rangle$ must satisfy:

$$K_3 \equiv C_{12} + C_{23} - C_{13} \leq 1 \quad \text{(LGI bound)}$$

Any classical stochastic process satisfies this inequality. **Quantum mechanics allows $K_3 > 1$** (up to a maximum of $K_3^{max} = 3/2$ for a qubit), signaling genuine quantum superposition across the temporal interval.

**Declared open programme [Tier 3/4] — Protocol A-Tertius.** (a) *Claimed:* a three-time LGI test, $K_3(\nu_c)>1$, on the singlet-triplet parity of a single radical pair read by ODMR with quantum-non-demolition weak measurements at $\tau_c=1/\nu_c\approx8.9$ ns spacing, registered as a binary gate (P.8c) for a protected-subspace coherence branch. (b) *Defects:* single-radical-pair singlet/triplet readout with QND measurements at ns spacing does not exist (radical-pair spin state is read destructively, through recombination yield on ensembles, and NV optical readout alone takes about 300 ns); and on the NV surrogate the test is non-diagnostic as designed, because LGI violation by a single NV spin is already established (Waldherr et al. 2011, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 107:090401) and any coherently precessing spin gives $K_3>1$ at suitable times. A positive result would therefore say nothing about a protected-subspace branch, and a null would depend on the chosen times. (c) *Closing would need:* an executable ensemble (ideal-negative-measurement) LGI scheme and a branch-specific prediction for $K_3(\nu)$ that differs from that of a bare coherent spin. (d) No decision, scorecard row or downstream claim uses A-Tertius as support; the former $K_3\approx1+\phi^{-9}/\pi$ effect size, sample-size plan and P.8c decision rule are withdrawn.

**13.4 Thermodynamic Constraints on Coherence**

**13.4.1 Conditional Landauer Bookkeeping for the Historical Rate Scenario**
Critics of quantum biology correctly identify **Thermal Noise** ($k_B T$) as the primary obstacle. At $310$ K, the decoherence time of a dipole in bulk water is $\tau_{raw} \approx 10^{-13}$ s; this bulk-water scale is a contextual control, not a measured tubulin-Trp $T_2$. Landauer's Principle bounds logically irreversible erasure; applying one erasure cost per hypothesized sampling update is an explicit scenario assumption, not evidence that a Zeno Drive samples, erases or operates at a particular rate.

**13.4.2 Heat-Sink Closure Status**

The thermodynamic heat-sink mechanism for the Zeno energy-balance remains an Open Problem; see App ORQ O.34.
Our thermodynamic audit compares two sampling regimes against the $\sim 1$ nW [Tier 3 — neurophysiology measurement] metabolic budget of a neuron. At $310$ K the Landauer erasure cost is $k_B T\ln 2 \approx 2.97\times10^{-21}$ J, so that budget supports about $3.4\times10^{11}$ logically irreversible bit erasures per second. An electronic-THz architecture that erases one bit per sampled degree of freedom per cycle is excluded even for a single bit. At $10^8$ Hz the same assumption permits at most $N_{\max}\approx3370$ independently erased bits; it does not select 100 MHz over lower frequencies. The 100 MHz figure is the historical material-scan scenario; it is not derived from this budget, nor from the recombination rate of §13.4.4 (a rate in s$^{-1}$, not a cyclic frequency). On the conditional $N_{\rm coop}=1.625\times10^9$ branch, one erasure per oscillator per cycle would require $1.625\times10^{17}$ erasures s$^{-1}$, exceeding the Landauer budget by $4.8\times10^5$. Even replacing the linear count by $\sqrt{N_{\rm coop}}$ would give $4.0\times10^4>3370$ and would not be justified by Landauer's linear bit-erasure accounting. The coherence protocol must therefore establish a QND/reversible implementation with an explicit reset and heat-sink budget; this thermodynamic closure remains open under O.34 [Tier 3].
**13.4.3 Tubulin Tryptophan Aromatic Radical Coherence**

Standard bulk water decoherence timescales ($\sim 10^{-13}$ s) define the thermal decoherence limit for unbound spins. The tubulin Trp radical-pair $T_2 \approx 1$-$10\ \mu$s baseline is a model-derived estimate (Tier 3) extrapolated from cryptochrome FAD-Trp radical-pair magnetoreception studies (Ritz et al. 2000; Hore & Mouritsen 2016) and microtubule radical-pair theoretical models (Zadeh-Haghighi & Simon 2022). Direct EPR/ODMR measurements on intact β-tubulin Trp radical pairs have NOT been performed; this direct measurement is a target of the in-vitro arm of Protocol A (Protocol A-Prime is the abiotic NV surrogate; App ORQ O.24 tracks the A-Prime enhancement amplitude). **Open Problem O.23** in Appendix ORQ asks whether a declared material model can extend a measured baseline toward the historical $\sim 10$ ms stress target (§13.4.4–§13.4.7, V1 §17.1.3c). No extension time is presently required by Selection or Level II.

**The Misra-Sudarshan formula and its applicability range [Tier 1 statement / Tier 3 application to Trp].**

The Misra-Sudarshan quantum Zeno theorem (Misra & Sudarshan 1977, *J. Math. Phys.* 18:756; reviewed in Facchi & Pascazio 2008, *J. Phys. A: Math. Theor.* 41:493001 §2.1) states that, for a quantum system prepared in $|\psi_0\rangle$ and subjected to projective measurements at interval $\tau_{Zeno}$, the effective decay rate in the short-interval limit is

$$\gamma_{\text{eff}}(\tau_{Zeno}) = \tau_{Zeno}/\tau_Z^2 \quad (\tau_{Zeno} \to 0),$$

where the **Zeno time** $\tau_Z$ is defined by the variance of the interaction Hamiltonian in the initial state:

$$\tau_Z^{-2}\equiv\frac{\sigma_H^2}{\hbar^2},\qquad \sigma_H^2\equiv\langle H^2\rangle-\langle H\rangle^2.$$
The survival-probability curvature fixes this time uniquely: $P(t)=1-(\sigma_Ht/\hbar)^2+O(t^4)$, hence $\tau_Z=\hbar/\sigma_H$. If the variance is quoted as a cyclic frequency $\Delta\nu_H\equiv\sigma_H/h$, then $\tau_Z=1/(2\pi\Delta\nu_H)$; using $1/\Delta\nu_H$ in the quadratic formula would inflate every squared-$\tau_Z$ result by $(2\pi)^2\approx39.5$.

Equivalently, the effective coherence time is $\tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau_Z^2/\tau_{Zeno}$. Within the manuscript's quadratic-to-exponential matching diagnostic, the nominal boundary is $\tau_{Zeno}<\tau^*\equiv\tau_Z^2/T_2$. Facchi, Nakazato & Pascazio (2001, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 86:2699, eq. 16) instead obtain $\tau^*\simeq\gamma_0\tau_Z^2$ for a golden-rule population-decay rate $\gamma_0$, while Facchi & Pascazio (2008 §2.2.5, eq. 19) define the crossover implicitly by $\gamma_{\rm eff}(\tau^*)=\gamma$. Identifying $\gamma_0=1/T_2$ is an additional Tier 3 channel-matching assumption, not a result of either source.

The Zeno time $\tau_Z$ and the exponential decoherence time $T_2$ are distinct physical quantities and *cannot in general be identified*: $\tau_Z$ is the convexity scale of the short-time survival probability (set by $\langle H_{int}^2 \rangle$), while $T_2$ is the long-time exponential dephasing rate (set by spin-bath spectral density). The two are related only via the crossover relation $\tau^* = \tau_Z^2/T_2$.

For the Trp radical-pair singlet, the relevant $H_{int}$ is the singlet–triplet hyperfine mixing $H_{hf}$. The engine's declared placeholder for the bare radical-pair variance is $\sigma_H/h = 30$ MHz (the FAD-derived rms of §13.1.2b; the TrpH$^{\bullet+}$ effective coupling $\approx27$ MHz would give $\tau_Z\approx5.85$ ns), so the Zeno time is

$$\tau_Z=\frac{1}{2\pi(30\,\text{MHz})}\approx5.305\ \text{ns}\quad\text{[Tier 3 — imported hyperfine placeholder].}$$

The bare Misra-Sudarshan formula evaluated at the canonical $\tau_{Zeno} = 10$ ns measurement benchmark gives

$$\tau_{\text{eff}}^{\text{bare MS}} = \tau_Z^2/\tau_{Zeno} \approx 2.8\ \text{ns}.$$

This **out-of-quadratic-regime diagnostic extrapolation of the single-pair bare Zeno formula is more than six orders below the $\sim 10$ ms declared stress target** used for Protocol A-Prime (§13.3.5) and Protocol D (Chapter 16), and more than four orders below the auxiliary $100\ \mu$s benchmark. The correct $\hbar/\sigma_H$ definition therefore widens rather than softens the disclosed coherence gap. The Misra-Sudarshan formula applied to the bare radical-pair Hamiltonian alone does *not* generate those declared material times. **Open Problem O.23** retains possible ordinary protected-subspace models (§13.4.5 dissipative-Zeno scaling; V1 §17.1.3c chiral phonon-polariton Decoherence-Free Subspace); none is a required or established GCT mechanism.

### 13.4.4 Spin-Selective Radical Recombination [Tier 3]

The candidate biochemical architecture is an ATP-coupled redox regeneration loop for Tryptophan (Trp) aromatic radical pairs in the β-tubulin hydrophobic pocket. The spin chemistry itself is standard radical-pair physics: the pair oscillates between Singlet (S) and Triplet (T) states via hyperfine coupling $\omega_{hf}$, and recombination back to the ground state is spin-selective, with the Singlet channel recombining at $\Gamma_{rec} \sim 10^8\ \text{s}^{-1}$ (a rate, not a 100 MHz cyclic frequency) while Triplet recombination is suppressed (Steiner & Ulrich 1989 *Chem. Rev.* 89:51). What is **not yet established** is the upstream biochemical pump that repeatedly prepares the Trp radical-pair state in tubulin under physiological conditions. ATP hydrolysis supplies the metabolic free-energy budget for the neural machinery and can in principle support local electron-transfer / redox cycling through mitochondrial and protein-pocket cofactors, but the explicit chain "ATP hydrolysis $\to$ electron donor/acceptor reset $\to$ beta-tubulin Trp radical-pair regeneration" is a closure target, not a demonstrated mechanism. The present chapter therefore treats ATP-coupled radical-pair regeneration as a **Tier 3 candidate mechanism** registered under App ORQ Open Problem O.34; the historical 100 MHz material-scan scenario was associated with the spin-selective recombination / hyperfine-mixing timescale ($\Gamma_{rec}\sim10^8$ s$^{-1}$), not with a derived ATP-hydrolysis clock. Even if measured in tubulin, a $10^8$ s$^{-1}$ recombination rate would be ordinary spin chemistry, not a Zeno Drive, phason rate or Selection-Operator clock.

A "WGM Cavity Bootstrap" reading of the historical 100 MHz material-scan scenario — in which an acoustic cavity resonance at 100 MHz is imagined to *project* the spin state — does not hold. A deterministic coherent coupling (cavity or otherwise) at 100 MHz produces reversible Rabi oscillations; its frequency is not a measurement rate. Strong unitary coupling can confine dynamics to Zeno subspaces (Facchi & Pascazio 2002, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 89:080401), but that protection scales with the coupling strength (e.g. the Rabi amplitude in spin locking), not with the drive frequency. The spin-selective recombination, by contrast, is a genuinely non-unitary Lindblad channel that depletes the singlet population irreversibly; the question is whether this Lindblad channel produces an *effective measurement* with strong-Zeno scaling in the parameter regime realized for Trp at 310 K.

**13.4.5 Lindblad Treatment of the Tubulin Trp Open Quantum System [Tier 3]**

To formalize the open-system dynamics of the Trp radical pair at 310 K we deploy the Lindblad master equation:

$$ \frac{d\rho}{dt} = -\frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{H}_{radical}, \rho] + \sum_i \gamma_i \mathcal{D}(\hat{\sigma}_-^i)\rho + \Gamma_{rec}\,\mathcal{D}(\hat{S}_{recomb})\rho, $$

where $\hat{H}_{radical}$ contains the hyperfine mixing $\omega_{hf}$, $\gamma_i = 1/T_2 \approx 10^5$ s$^{-1}$ [Tier 3 — model-derived import/extrapolation from cryptochrome/FAD-Trp radical-pair systems and microtubule radical-pair models; direct β-tubulin EPR/ODMR remains O.24] is the bare thermal spin decay, and $\Gamma_{rec} \sim 10^8$ s$^{-1}$ is the spin-selective recombination rate [Tier 3 — Trp radical-pair chemistry]. Here $\mathcal{D}(\hat{L})\rho = \hat{L}\rho\hat{L}^\dagger - \tfrac{1}{2}\{\hat{L}^\dagger\hat{L},\rho\}$, and $\hat{S}_{recomb}$ is defined as a jump from the radical-pair spin manifold into an explicit product/sink state (e.g. $|P\rangle\langle S|$, with channel sums when singlet/triplet products are both retained). On the enlarged radical-plus-product Hilbert space this is a CPTP Lindblad evolution; if the product/sink sector is traced out and only unrecombined radical pairs are retained, the reduced radical-pair equation is a trace-nonincreasing conditional master equation.

In the strong-coupling continuous-measurement limit (Facchi & Pascazio 2008 *J. Phys. A* 41:493001 §13.3), a dissipator of rate $\Gamma$ pinning a system into one of its eigenspaces suppresses leakage from the protected subspace at a rate $\gamma_{\text{leak}} \sim |\Omega|^2/\Gamma$, where $\Omega$ is the off-diagonal coupling to the leakage channel. **Unit guard:** $\Delta E/h$ and $\nu$ are cyclic frequencies in Hz; $\Omega$ and Lindblad rates are angular rates in rad/s. The dissipative-Zeno comparison converts with $\Omega=2\pi\nu$ before comparing or squaring rates. Applied to the Trp recombination dissipator with $\Omega = \omega_{hf} = 2\pi\times30\ \text{MHz} \approx 1.9\times10^8$ rad/s (the $\Delta_{ST}/h \approx 30$ MHz hyperfine band) and $\Gamma = \Gamma_{rec} \sim 10^8$ s$^{-1}$, this gives

$$ \tau_{\text{eff}}^{\text{dissipative}} = 1/\gamma_{\text{leak}} \sim \Gamma_{rec}/\omega_{hf}^2 \approx 2.8\times10^{-9}\ \text{s} = 2.8\ \text{ns}, $$

i.e., the dissipative-Zeno scaling recovers the same order-of-magnitude estimate as the pulsed Misra-Sudarshan formula of §13.4.3. The radical-pair recombination channel acting alone — whether interpreted as a continuous Lindblad dissipator or as a stroboscopic projective measurement — does *not* extend the Trp coherence time toward the historical 10 ms material-coherence target used for Protocol A-Prime / Protocol D. That target is not a Selection or Level-II requirement.

**Historical shielding-factor scenario.** The Trp radical pair lives inside the hydrophobic pocket and is not in bulk water; its decoherence channel is the singlet-triplet hyperfine interaction, with a model-derived imported baseline $T_2 \approx 1$–$10\ \mu$s extrapolated from cryptochrome/FAD-Trp systems and radical-pair models rather than direct β-tubulin measurement (Ritz et al. 2000; Hore & Mouritsen 2016; O.24). Taking $\tau_{\text{target}} \sim 10$ ms as a declared stress-test target defines

$$S_{\text{required}} = \frac{\tau_{\text{target}}}{\tau_{\text{base}}} \;\sim\; \frac{10^{-2}\ \text{s}}{10^{-5}\ \text{s}} = 10^3,$$
where $\tau_{\text{base}}\approx10\,\mu$s is the imported radical-pair baseline (distinct from the bulk-water $\tau_{raw}\approx10^{-13}$ s of §13.4.1).

The resulting $10^3$ ratio is arithmetic for that chosen material scenario. The $\sim 10^9$ shielding figure occasionally quoted in the warm-decoherence literature instead compares with an unrelated bulk-water scale ($\tau_{bulk} \sim 10^{-13}$ s). Neither is a GCT or Polaron requirement. **Open Problem O.23** asks whether any specified ordinary protected-subspace model can produce a measured enhancement and with what mechanism (§13.4.6, V1 §17.1.3c).

**13.4.6 Partial Floquet-Lindblad Master Equation for the Open Zeno System [Tier 3]**

The Misra-Sudarshan formula isolates the quantum system from its environment. To model the Tubulin Trp radical pair at 310 K coupled to a protein spin bath, the current manuscript uses a **partial Floquet-Lindblad scaffold** for the open quantum system (OQS) density matrix $\rho$:

$$ \frac{d\rho}{dt} = -\frac{i}{\hbar}[H_{\rm eff}, \rho] + \sum_k \left( L_k \rho L_k^\dagger - \frac{1}{2}\{L_k^\dagger L_k, \rho\} \right) $$

where the Floquet driving Hamiltonian $H_F(t)$ is defined in energy units:
$$ H_F(t) = \frac{\Delta_{ST}}{2}\sigma_z + \hbar\Omega \cos(2\pi \nu_d t)\sigma_x, $$
with $\Delta_{ST}=h(30\,\mathrm{MHz})$ (so $\Delta_{ST}/h \approx 30 \text{ MHz}$) as the engine's bare singlet-triplet placeholder (an FAD-derived external material-scenario input, §13.1.2b) and $\nu_d$ as an applied laboratory drive frequency. Equivalently, in angular-frequency units one may write $K_F(t)=H_F(t)/\hbar=(\Delta_{ST}/2\hbar)\sigma_z+\Omega\cos(2\pi\nu_d t)\sigma_x$ and $d\rho/dt=-i[K_{\rm eff},\rho]+\cdots$. The Protocol A-Prime branch frequencies ($112 \pm 10$ MHz if O.12 closes positive; $42 \pm 10$ MHz if O.12 closes negative) are historical proxy scan windows, not replacements for the bare $\Delta_{ST}$ value; O.12 does not establish them as operating frequencies, and they are not GCT rates.

The environmental spin-bath coupling retained in this scaffold is defined by three Lindblad jump operators $L_k$:
* $L_1 = \sqrt{\gamma_\uparrow}\sigma_+$ (Thermal excitation)
* $L_2 = \sqrt{\gamma_\downarrow}\sigma_-$ (Thermal relaxation)
* $L_3 = \sqrt{\gamma_\phi/2}\sigma_z$ (Pure dephasing)

where detailed balance enforces $\gamma_\uparrow / \gamma_\downarrow = \exp(-\Delta_{ST} / k_B T)$ — $\Delta_{ST}$ is an energy, so no factor of $\hbar$ enters; written with the angular splitting $\omega_{ST} = \Delta_{ST}/\hbar$ the same relation reads $\exp(-\hbar\omega_{ST}/k_B T)$ — and the dephasing rate is $\gamma_\phi = 1/T_2^*$. 

This is not the full dissipator set for hydrated tubulin at 310 K. Omitted channels include spin-selective singlet/triplet recombination into explicit product/sink states, hyperfine anisotropy, dipolar nuclear-spin bath fluctuations, vibrational relaxation of the Trp pocket, solvent/electrostatic noise, and any O.23 chiral-phonon-polariton dark-state jump structure. Those channels are closure targets for O.23/O.24 and for the in-vitro arm of Protocol A, not solved inputs here.

Within this declared open-system scaffold, transforming into the Floquet rotating frame defines a **scenario factor** $\eta_F \in (0, 1]$ for the effective coherence time of the open driven system:
$$ \tau_{eff}^F = \tau_{eff}^{\text{bare}} \cdot \eta_F \quad \text{where} \quad \eta_F = \frac{1}{1 + (\gamma_\phi \cdot T_2)^2 \cdot f(\Delta_{drive} / \Delta_{ST})}, $$

where the baseline $\tau_{eff}^{\text{bare}}$ must be supplied by a measured or separately justified material model (the bare Misra-Sudarshan estimate of §13.4.3 alone gives $\tau_{eff}^{\text{bare MS}} \approx 2.8$ ns from radical-pair recombination, well below the $\sim 10$ ms declared stress target; a hypothetical chiral phonon-polariton Decoherence-Free Subspace lifetime $\tau_{eff}^{\text{DFS}}$ of V1 §17.1.3c could be inserted only as an O.23 scenario). Inserting either baseline produces scenario curves only; no baseline is currently a GCT coherence-extension mechanism.

At ordinary driven two-level resonance, $\nu_d = \Delta_{ST}/h$, the declared detuning function gives $f \to 0$ and $\eta_F \to 1$; off resonance the Floquet factor multiplicatively suppresses the supplied baseline. This conditional algebra does not identify a geometric or phason resonance.

**13.4.7 The Zeno/Anti-Zeno Crossover and the Drive-Window Logic [Tier 3; declared open programme]**

**Conditional open-system comparison:**

Within the short-time quadratic model used above, equating the effective rate $\gamma_{\rm eff}(\tau_{Zeno}) = \tau_{Zeno}/\tau_Z^2$ to the bare exponential rate $1/T_2$ locates a crossover at $\tau^* = \tau_Z^2/T_2$, equivalently the crossover frequency

$$\nu^* = 1/\tau^* = T_2/\tau_Z^2.$$

This identity is a diagnostic of that model rather than a general result. Misra & Sudarshan (1977) establish the limiting Zeno dynamics, and Facchi & Pascazio (2008 §2.2.5) define a Zeno/anti-Zeno crossover implicitly by $\gamma_{\rm eff}(\tau^*) = \gamma$ where a finite intersection exists; neither identifies $\tau^*$ with $\tau_Z^2/T_2$ nor guarantees anti-Zeno behaviour beyond it, and the same review cautions that $\tau_Z$ can misestimate the quadratic interval. The sign assignments below therefore hold only within the quadratic model and its assumed spectral density. Here $\nu_{\text{sample}}$ is the rate of a physical measurement channel (for example recombination); an applied drive frequency is not such a rate.

- For $\nu_{\text{sample}} > \nu^*$ (Zeno regime): measurement *suppresses* decoherence — coherence time increases.
- For $\nu_{\text{sample}} < \nu^*$ (Anti-Zeno regime): measurement *accelerates* decoherence — coherence time decreases.

The crossover depends on both the Zeno time $\tau_Z$ (variance of the interaction Hamiltonian) and the exponential dephasing time $T_2$. For Tubulin Trp radical pairs with the engine's $\sigma_H/h \approx 30$ MHz placeholder [Tier 3 — FAD-derived rms placeholder, within ~10% of the TrpH$^{\bullet+}$ effective coupling, §13.1.2b] and $T_2 \approx 10\ \mu$s [Tier 3 — model-derived extrapolation from cryptochrome FAD-Trp radical-pair magnetoreception studies (Ritz 2000, Hore-Mouritsen 2016) plus microtubule radical-pair theoretical models (Zadeh-Haghighi-Simon 2022); direct β-tubulin Trp EPR/ODMR measurement is the closure target of O.24], $\tau_Z\approx5.305$ ns and the quadratic-model bare crossover is $\nu^*\approx3.6\times10^{11}$ Hz, far above the recombination rate $\Gamma_{rec}\approx10^8$ s$^{-1}$. This places the bare single-pair recombination channel in the inverse-Zeno regime relative to the bare hyperfine Hamiltonian, consistent with the §13.4.3 conclusion that the radical-pair channel alone does not extend coherence.

**Declared open programme [Tier 3] — drive-frequency Zeno windows.** (a) *Claimed:* that the applied RF/coil drive frequency $\nu_d$ acts as the Zeno sampling rate, so that the registered A-Prime branch windows sit on the inverse-Zeno side of the bare $\nu^*$ and would move to the Zeno-protected side if an O.23 protected subspace lowered $\nu^*_{\rm DFS}$ into the kHz–MHz band; an in-vitro comparison of $T_2$ in the branch windows against a 50 kHz drive was registered as a decision on the O.23 layer. The mapping $\nu_d\to\nu_{\text{sample}}$ is a category error, so no Zeno/anti-Zeno side can be assigned to any drive window, and the 50 kHz comparison decides nothing. (c) *Closing would need:* a physical measurement channel whose rate is tuned (e.g. an optical or recombination readout rate), or a restatement of the windows as dynamical-decoupling filter frequencies probing a noise spectral density. (d) Nothing downstream uses this window logic as support: the A-Prime F1 rule (§13.3.5) does not depend on it, and the former 50 kHz decision rule is withdrawn.

**13.5 The $\eta_{Zeno}$ Scenario Calculator (Formal Definition; Non-Diagnostic)**

The preceding sections specified the Zeno Drive piece by piece — the Tavis-Cummings cooperative pumping condition (§13.1.2), the candidate magnon-polaron origin of the registered $\nu_c \approx 112 \pm 10$ MHz window (§13.1.2b; Tier 3 specific value), the open-system Floquet-Lindblad correction (§13.4.6), and the substrate-discriminant logic of Protocol A-Prime (§13.3.5). The present section assembles these into a single closed-form procedure that returns a scalar $\eta_{Zeno}$ given a substrate description, so that past engine outputs remain reproducible. It computes a dimensionless rate ratio **only after all rates and model gates are supplied externally**; it does not derive a GCT material coupling, classify a substrate or measure consciousness.

**The DMC legs are candidate missing-leg controls, not an Apperception gate.** Non-zero nuclear spin and molecular chirality motivate controlled comparisons for the proposed phason channel, but neither is established as necessary or sufficient for Level II. The former $g_{single}^{eff}$, $\eta_{Zeno}$, and $N_{coh}$ numbers are retained below only as conditional external-rate scenarios. O.21, O.23, O.34, a typed and normalized coupling, and an independent phenomenal bridge remain open; therefore no material descriptor executes a zero-coupling inference or categorical substrate verdict.

The procedure is computationally **tractable** on a small set of declared substrate variables — nuclear-spin lattice, molecular chirality, Tavis-Cummings cavity parameters, vibrational thermal-bath coupling — and is implemented as a chain of five documented functions in the GCT Physics Engine (`GCT_Physics_Engine/src/protocol_eta_zeno.py`), a conditional scenario calculator whose outputs audit supplied arithmetic rather than physical verdicts. with $E_{\text{phason}}^{\,zpf} = \sqrt{\hbar\,\omega_d / (2\,m_{eff})}$ (as written this has units of velocity, not electric field, so the displayed form is dimensionally incomplete and does not define a rate) and $\chi \in \{+1,-1,0\}$ the chirality sign ($\chi = 0$ for achiral in this scenario form; a missing-leg control, not a derived zero coupling). **13.5.8 Computational Tractability and Its Limit**

The IIT integrated-information $\Phi$ is *defined* over the system's $2^{N-1} - 1$ bipartitions (Oizumi-Albantakis-Tononi 2014), a search whose cost grows exponentially with $N$ and is intractable for any realistic biological network in closed form. The rate-ratio calculator above is computationally cheap because it accepts a small supplied tuple — nuclear-spin lattice, chirality, Tavis-Cummings cavity parameters $(g, N, \kappa)$, and the open-system parameters $(\gamma_\phi, T_2, \Delta_{ST})$. That does not make it a replacement for IIT $\Phi$ or a computation of the Selection Operator $\mathcal{F}_{sel}$: the physical coupling and the independent Level-II bridge remain missing, and the Tier 3 substrate inputs (Trp dipole moment, $N_{bio}$ estimate, NV coupling parameters) carry their own uncertainty.

Protocol A-Prime (§13.3.5) can tighten ordinary material-response parameters against bench measurement. The same data alone cannot determine the missing GCT vertices, physical coefficient, normalization, material embedding or rate.

The chain is mirrored in `GCT_Physics_Engine/src/protocol_eta_zeno.py`. The reference implementation evaluates a declared scenario, returns the per-step quantities, and writes the canonical regression artifact `GCT_Physics_Engine/data/protocol_eta_zeno_results.json`; it is not a substrate-decision engine.

**Scope of the procedure:**

1. **Steps 2–5 are exact conditional algebra given their inputs; Step 1 ($g_{single}$) is a quarantined Tier 3 scenario form; all numerical inputs are Tier 3, and the GCT rate input is undefined.** Each step is fixed once the registered substrate tuple is supplied (Tavis-Cummings ratio, V3 §13.1.2; Floquet-Lindblad open-system scaffold, V3 §13.4.6; Dual Material Constraint candidate controls, V1 §16.2.6). Apart from the external 38.3 MHz scenario anchor, the chain has no fitted numerical knob, and its absolute outputs inherit the Tier 3 substrate inputs listed below. Exact evaluation does not validate the tuple.
2. **The numerical values are conditional external-rate scenarios.** Molecular and NV parameters are imported, but the phason coupling is neither typed nor measured. The central O.21-pending inventory remains $n_{rp}=0$ and the $n_{rp}=1$ value is sensitivity bookkeeping. DMC descriptors define candidate controls; they do not force qualitative substrate logic or a Level-II verdict.
3. **The procedure is tractable on the test cases but is NOT experimentally validated against bench measurements.** CISS, phonon, spin, population and linewidth measurements are material proxies, not measurements of a GCT coupling. Protocol A-Prime (§13.3.5) can support or reject the registered proxy pattern; it cannot promote the historical rate chain. Its registered pattern is a non-monotonic peak in either the Branch A $112\pm10$ MHz or Branch B $42\pm10$ MHz hypothesis region plus chirality contrast after the full half-open $[32,200)$ MHz scan at $\Delta\nu_{\rm step}\leq1/(4\pi T_{2,\rm eff})$ in NV-centre + chiral h-BN samples. The 150 MHz sign is unfixed and recorded separately as an O.23/O.24 diagnostic outside F1/Holm. A positive branch/chirality result plus the chirality-reversal control of §13.3.5.A would constrain the Tier 3 material inputs and measure the peak amplitude that Open Problems O.23 + O.24 leave conditional; empirical agreement does not promote those inputs to Tier 2. Promotion requires an independent derivation of the relevant DFS/chiral parameters and normalized physical coupling from the declared premises, closing the O.23/O.24 derivational dependencies without fitting the target measurements. Observations then test the resulting claim.
4. **The procedure does NOT replace Protocol A-Prime and is not a substrate-decision engine.** Its output on a substrate is a scenario number, not a prediction of what a Protocol A-Prime measurement must find and not a substitute for the measurement. The Dual Material Constraint is not established as necessary or sufficient, and $\eta_{Zeno}$ supplies no Level-II verdict.

**Cross-references.** V1 §17.10 and §16.2.6 cite this procedure as a candidate-control and conditional-scenario audit, not a substrate decision rule. V3 §13.3.5 supplies a bench material-proxy test; the typed coupling (V1 Ch17 §17.1.3) and an independent phenomenal bridge remain open.
