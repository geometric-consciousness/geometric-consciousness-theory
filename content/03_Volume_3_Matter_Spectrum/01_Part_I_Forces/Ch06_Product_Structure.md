**6.2 Orthogonality of Interaction Sectors**

**6.2.1 Theorem 6.1 [Tier 1 mathematics; Tier 3 application]: Sector Independence**

**Theorem 6.1 (mathematical statement) [Tier 1]:** *Let Lie algebras of transformations act on mutually independent factors of a configuration space (for example on orthogonal subspaces of $\mathbb{R}^6$, on distinct tensor factors of a fiber, or on a scalar phase), each acting trivially on the other factors. Then generators belonging to different factors commute, and the joint algebra is their direct sum. This fixes only the local Lie-algebra structure: the global gauge group may still carry a discrete-center quotient, as in the Standard Model form $[SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)]/\mathbb{Z}_6$, which the commuting-generator calculation does not fix.*

**Application [Tier 3, conditional on the sector placement].** In this volume's bookkeeping the color candidate acts on the three-fold-axis orientation of the RT window in $E_\perp$ (an open Tier 3 candidate, V3 Ch03), the weak candidate acts on the internal $2I$-frame bundle that is also placed on the $E_\perp$ side (Tier 3 placement, §6.1.2; V3 Ch02 §2.1.1), and the electromagnetic $U(1)$ acts on the condensate phase $\theta$. Theorem 6.1 then gives two commutation results: spatial-frame rotations in $E_\parallel$ commute with every transformation acting only on $E_\perp$, and all of these commute with the scalar phase rotation. It does **not** give the commutation of the weak and color generators. Both are placed on the internal side, so the orthogonality of $E_\parallel$ and $E_\perp$ does not separate them. The commutation of the weak and color generators is then an assumption of the Tier 3 placement (that the $2I$ frame and the three-fold-axis orientation are independent factors of the internal fiber), not a consequence of Theorem 6.1.

**6.2.2 Proof: The Dimensional Sum Rule**

The proof uses the dimensional decomposition of the parent space ($3+3=6$) and the scalar character of the phase.
1. **Independent factors commute:** If $X$ acts on factor $A$ as $X\otimes\mathbf{1}$ and $Y$ acts on factor $B$ as $\mathbf{1}\otimes Y$, then $[X\otimes\mathbf{1},\,\mathbf{1}\otimes Y]=0$. The physical and internal manifolds are orthogonal subspaces of $\mathbb{R}^6$, so they supply such a factorization ($E_\parallel \perp E_\perp$): a rotation of a defect's Burgers vector in $E_\perp$ leaves its orientation in $E_\parallel$ invariant, and conversely. Under the §6.1.2 placement this separates spatial rotations from the internal (color and weak) transformations, but not color from weak.
2. **Phase Independence:** The superfluid phase $\theta$ is a scalar field. Geometric rotations of the frame (either internal or physical) commute with the global $U(1)$ phase rotation.

Because these operations act on independent degrees of freedom, the commutators between generators of the separated factors vanish. This proves local independence at the Lie-algebra level for those factors. The remaining relation $[T_{SU(3)}, T_{SU(2)}] = 0$ holds only under the Tier 3 assumption, stated in §6.2.1, that color and weak act on independent factors of the internal fiber. Neither step rules out a finite shared-center quotient in the global gauge group; that global-topology question is carried separately.

**6.2.3 Symmetry vs. Interaction: The Higgs Bridge**
At the Lie-algebra/local-sector level the symmetry actions are taken to be product-separated (by the $E_\parallel \perp E_\perp$ orthogonality, and between weak and color by the Tier 3 internal-factor assumption of §6.2.1), while the physical excitations couple through the **shared vacuum condensate $\Psi$** and the **Higgs dilation mode**. Forces interact not because their local generators mix, but because they all modulate the same Higgs-tensioned 6D substrate; any discrete-center quotient in the global gauge group remains a separate global-identification question.
