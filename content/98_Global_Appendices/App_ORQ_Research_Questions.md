**ORQ.1 Mathematical Proof**

1. **N-Agent Consensus Protocol Convergence — conditional Tier 1 Perron–Frobenius theorem; physical kernel open** (engines: `GCT_Physics_Engine/src/protocol_h1_1_consensus_convergence.py`, `GCT_Physics_Engine/src/protocol_h1_1b_krein_rutman_profinite.py`). For a **declared** finite matrix $W$ that is row-stochastic and strictly positive, Perron–Frobenius for strictly positive matrices (Horn & Johnson 1985 Thm 8.2.11, Cor 8.2.12) gives: (i) spectral radius 1 with simple leading eigenvalue $\lambda_1 = 1$; (ii) $|\lambda_k| < 1$ for all other eigenvalues; (iii) geometric convergence at rate $|\lambda_2|^t$ to the unique fixed point $\Phi^*$; (iv) $\Phi^*$ is the §11.3.2 weighted-mean stationary state. This is a standard Tier 1 conditional theorem. Its GCT application rests on three separate premises: (a) the entropic term of Ch11 §11.3.2 has been reduced so that the pure weighted-mean update $\Phi_i^{(t+1)} = \sum_j W_{ij} \Phi_j^{(t)}$, $W_{ij} = \kappa_{ij}/\sum_k \kappa_{ik}$, is the operative dynamics; (b) a chosen address filtration and metric $d_H$ have been supplied; and (c) the address realization and the positive coupling kernel $\kappa_{ij}$ are physically derived rather than declared as $\kappa_{ij} = \kappa_0 e^{-\lambda_c d_H}$. Shared cylinders alone imply none of these couplings. Sparse, reducible, periodic or state-dependent networks require their own primitivity/ergodicity analysis and need not converge to one consensus state. Numerical verification across six representative declared networks (p ∈ {2, 3}, depth ∈ {2, 3, 4}, $\lambda_c$ ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 0.7, 2.0}) passes all four theorem points with eigenvalue identities at $\sim 10^{-16}$ relative precision and exponential convergence at the predicted geometric rate. The infinite-resolution statement is conditional in the same way. On a selected $p$-primary factor or chosen cofinal filtration, the normalized kernel operator $(K f)(s) = \int \kappa(s, t) f(t) d\mu(t) / Z(s)$ on $L^2(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_p, d\mu)$ is Hilbert-Schmidt for the declared bounded kernel (since $|\kappa(s,t)| \le \kappa_0$ and $\mu(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_p) = 1$ give $\|\kappa\|_2^2 \le \kappa_0^2 < \infty$), hence compact (Reed-Simon 1980 Vol 1 Thm VI.22); strict positivity of that kernel makes $K$ strongly positive, so Krein-Rutman (Krein & Rutman 1948; Deimling 1985 Thm 19.3 + Cor 19.4) gives a simple leading eigenvalue, and a spectral gap then gives the geometric rate. Numerical verification across depth $\in \{4, 6, 8, 10\}$ (N up to 1024) finds the spectral gap stabilising at $1 - |\lambda_2| = 0.832910$ (relative spread $1.8 \times 10^{-3}$ across depths; HS-norm final-two-depths relative difference $2 \times 10^{-6}$). These calculations verify finite truncations of the chosen kernel family; they are not a topology-only theorem about the universal all-prime Solenoid or a proof that a physical N-Agent network realizes it. The singular limit $\lambda_c \to \infty$ (physically uncoupled agents) lies outside the theorem. **Disposition:** Tier 1 given the operator hypotheses; Tier 3 for the GCT address/coupling realization. Convergence of a declared update does not by itself show that shared reality is a consensus render among agents; that physical reading remains a Tier 3 analogy pending the coupling derivation.
