**QLA.2 The Discrete Geometric Action**

In standard LQCD, spacetime is discretized as a hypercubic lattice, with fermion fields $\psi$ existing at the nodes and gauge fields $U_\mu \in SU(3)$ existing as parallel transporters (links) along the strictly orthogonal edges.

In QLQCD, the physical vacuum is an Ammann-Kramer-Neri (AKN) 3D tiling generated by the projection of $\mathbb{Z}^6$. The fundamental unit cells are **Rhombohedra**, not cubes (specifically, prolate and oblate rhombohedra formed by the 6 canonical basis vectors $\mathbf{e}_i$ of the icosahedral star).

**QLA.2.1 Gauge Links on Rhombohedral Edges**
Gauge links $U_i(x)$ are defined exclusively on the edges of the rhombohedra. Because the AKN tiling has 6 independent generator directions, the index $i \in \{1, \dots, 6\}$. 

The standard Wilson action is replaced by the **Icosahedral Plaquette Action**:
$$ S_{gauge} = \frac{\beta}{3} \sum_{\text{plaquettes } p} \mathrm{Re}\left[ \mathrm{Tr} (1 - U_p) \right] $$
where a plaquette $p$ is a rhombus face rather than a square of the 3D unit cells. The path-ordered product $U_p = U_1 U_2 U_3^\dagger U_4^\dagger$ traces the perimeter of the rhombus.

**QLA.3 The Matching Rule Constraint (Monte Carlo Updates)**

The most profound algorithmic departure from standard LQCD lies in the Monte Carlo (MC) update constraints. In hypercubic LQCD, local link variables are updated independently and configurations are sampled with weight $e^{-S}$.

The canonical AKN tiling permits a small finite set of distinct local neighbourhoods around any node, classified by how the cut-and-project window intersects the 12 nearest-neighbour displacements of $\mathbb{Z}^6$ projected into $E_\perp$. The count depends on what information is called a "vertex configuration"; three conventions are kept separate throughout the manuscript: the historical literature count **24** (App CXL §CXL.3), the bare nearest-neighbour count **9** and the radius-two decorated count **101** (both below). No vertex-star count of 32 is used. A bare $Z=20$ star is impossible in this twelve-displacement alphabet; where a $Z=20$ object is used elsewhere, it must denote a larger-radius or decorated cluster. With matching-rule decorations enforcing aperiodicity, the decorated-star count is larger. Under the cut-and-project identity that a matching-rule decoration is the radius-2 local window patch (the next-shell data that fixes a vertex's admissible nearest-neighbour updates: $v+d_k$ present iff $\pi_\perp(v)+\pi_\perp(d_k)\in W$, and $v+d_k+d_j$ present iff $\pi_\perp(v)+\pi_\perp(d_k)+\pi_\perp(d_j)\in W$), the radius-2 patch is a function of the perp-coordinate alone, so the decorated stars are the cells of the partition of $W$ by the radius-2 ball of window translates. **QLA.3.1 The Algorithmic Bottleneck**
A Monte Carlo trajectory updating the gauge field $U_i$ or the phason displacement field $\delta \mathbf{w}$ must be rejected if it creates a vertex star outside of the allowed set $\mathcal{V}_{\text{AKN}}$.
* **Hypercubic MC:** Unconstrained local updates.
* **QLQCD MC:** Proposed constrained graph-rewriting. An update is intended to represent a localized **phason flip** (a De Bruijn dual surface passing through a node).

The algorithm requires a Metropolis-Hastings acceptance step where the proposal distribution $Q(x \to x')$ includes a binary viability check:
$$ \mathcal{P}_{accept} = \min \left( 1, e^{-\Delta S} \right) \times \prod_{v \in \text{update zone}} \Theta(v \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{AKN}}) $$
where $\Theta$ is an indicator function returning 1 if the updated vertex $v$ belongs to the set of legal AKN stars $\mathcal{V}_{\text{AKN}}$, and 0 otherwise. This is a proposed algorithmic matching-rule constraint; it is not yet a derivation of a physical $K_\perp$, proposal measure, flip path, rate or mobility [Tier 3 dynamics open].

**QLA.4 The Fermion Doubling Problem: Proposed Evasion [Tier 3 conjecture]**

A historic plague of Lattice QCD is the **Fermion Doubling Problem**. The Nielsen-Ninomiya theorem proves that any local, translationally invariant, hermitian discretization of fermions on a periodic lattice inevitably produces spurious "ghost" particles (2$^D$ fermions for every 1 intended fermion). Standard LQCD patches this by introducing "Wilson fermions," explicitly breaking chiral symmetry by adding an ad-hoc mass term that diverges in the continuum limit, forcing the ghosts infinitely massive.

**QLQCD is proposed to evade the Nielsen-Ninomiya theorem [Tier 3 conjecture].** Evading a theorem's hypotheses is not the same as proving its conclusion false: the argument below shows only that the theorem does not apply as stated, not that the quasicrystalline discretization is free of doublers.

**QLA.4.1 The Chiral Cut**
The underlying cause of fermion doubling is the discrete translation symmetry of the hypercubic lattice, which causes the momentum-space Dirac operator to have multiple zeros at the edges of the Brillouin zone ($p = \pi/a$). 

By definition, the AKN tiling is generated by an **irrational cut** ($\lambda = \phi$). It is quasiperiodic (a model set). **It possesses no discrete translational symmetry.** Therefore there is no ordinary Brillouin zone, and the lattice periodicity assumed by the Nielsen-Ninomiya theorem is absent at the lattice scale [Tier 1 observation about the theorem's hypotheses].

**QLA.4.2 Doubler-Absence Conjecture [Tier 3/open]**
The proposed GCT Dirac operator $D_{GCT}$ is defined on the aperiodic grid:
$$ \sum_{\mu=1}^6 \gamma^\mu \nabla_\mu^{(AKN)} \psi(x) = 0 $$
The heuristic expectation is that, because the discrete derivative $\nabla_\mu^{(AKN)}$ operates along the 6 aperiodic axes, the spectral weight of $D_{GCT}$ near zero is concentrated at the physical origin $p = 0$, and the 15 other parity-inverted momentum modes that plague hypercubic lattices ($\mathbf{p} \propto \pi$) have no counterpart in the quasicrystal. This is not established. The earlier wording, that "the icosahedral projection is inherently chiral", is also incorrect as stated: the full point group $I_h$ contains the inversion, and only its rotation subgroup $I$ is chiral. Showing the absence of doublers requires a spectral analysis of the quasiperiodic operator $D_{GCT}$ (its near-zero modes and their continuum limit), together with a check of locality and of the chiral (Ginsparg-Wilson-type) structure.

**Conditional conclusion:** if the conjecture holds, QLQCD would need no Wilson mass term and would preserve chiral symmetry for massless fermions on the quasicrystalline lattice. Until the spectral analysis is done, the doubling problem is an open item for QLQCD rather than a solved one.
