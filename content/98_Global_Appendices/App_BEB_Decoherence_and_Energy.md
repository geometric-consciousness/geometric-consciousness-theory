### BEB.2b — Coherence-Extension Material Scenario [Tier 1 Misra–Sudarshan arithmetic; Tier 3 material scenario; O.23 open]

The Misra-Sudarshan quantum Zeno theorem (Misra & Sudarshan 1977 *J. Math. Phys.* 18:756; reviewed in Facchi & Pascazio 2008 *J. Phys. A* 41:493001 §2.1) gives, for projective measurements at interval $\tau_{\rm meas}$, a diagnostic effective-coherence-time extrapolation from the short-time survival law:

$$\tau_{eff} = \frac{\tau_Z^2}{\tau_{\rm meas}}, \qquad \tau_Z^{-2}\equiv\frac{\sigma_H^2}{\hbar^2},\qquad \sigma_H^2\equiv\langle H^2\rangle-\langle H\rangle^2.$$

The Zeno time is uniquely fixed by the survival-probability curvature: $P(t)=1-(\sigma_Ht/\hbar)^2+O(t^4)$, hence $\tau_Z=\hbar/\sigma_H$. Writing the variance as a cyclic frequency $\Delta\nu_H\equiv\sigma_H/h$ gives $\tau_Z=1/(2\pi\Delta\nu_H)$, not $1/\Delta\nu_H$. For a finite projective-measurement interval, $\Gamma_{\rm eff}=\tau_{\rm meas}/\tau_Z^2$ is the standard short-time consequence of the Misra-Sudarshan limit (see Facchi & Pascazio 2008 §2.1). Within the manuscript's quadratic-to-exponential matching diagnostic, the nominal boundary $\tau^*=\tau_Z^2/T_2$ is $2.81$ ps for $T_2=10\,\mu$s and $\tau_Z=5.305$ ns, so the 10 ns recombination interval is an out-of-regime diagnostic extrapolation. Facchi, Nakazato & Pascazio (2001, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 86:2699, eq. 16) instead write $\tau^*\simeq\gamma_0\tau_Z^2$ for a golden-rule population-decay rate $\gamma_0$; identifying $\gamma_0=1/T_2$ is an additional Tier 3 channel-matching assumption.

where the **Zeno time** $\tau_Z$ is set by the variance of the interaction Hamiltonian in the initial state — *not* the exponential decoherence time $T_2$. Facchi & Pascazio (2008 §2.2.5, eq. 19) define a finite Zeno/inverse-Zeno crossover implicitly by $\gamma_{\rm eff}(\tau^*)=\gamma$ when an intersection exists; they do not identify it generally with $\tau_Z^2/T_2$ or guarantee the sign beyond it.

### BEB.11.1 Spin-Orbit Coupling in Helical Peptide Bonds

$$\mathbf{B}_{SO} = \frac{\hbar}{2 m_e c^2 e} (\nabla V \times \mathbf{k})$$

The CISS effect amplifies this spin-orbit field through the collective helical geometry. For a peptide helix with $n_{res}$ residues and pitch $p$, the spin-split conductance is (Naaman et al.):
