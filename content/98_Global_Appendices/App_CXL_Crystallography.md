### **Appendix CXL: Crystallography of 6D Lattices**

**CXL.1 The Rank-Six Icosahedral Bravais Setting and the $E_8$ Connection**

GCT conditionally adopts the primitive rank-six icosahedral Bravais lattice $P\cong\mathbb Z^6$ as its physical-vacuum architecture [Tier 1/2 structural postulate]. Full three-dimensional icosahedral indexing fixes rank 6 but does not fix the Bravais centring: the classified alternatives are primitive $P\cong\mathbb Z^6$, face-centred $F\cong D_6$, and body-centred $I\cong D_6^*$. The primitive-$P$ choice is therefore an explicit architectural adoption, not a consequence of an embedding in $E_8$.

The **$D_6$ Root Lattice** (the checkerboard lattice) is a sublattice of $\mathbb{Z}^6$ defined by the parity constraint:
$$ D_6 = \left\{ \mathbf{x} = (x_1, \dots, x_6) \in \mathbb{Z}^6 : \sum_{i=1}^6 x_i \equiv 0 \pmod 2 \right\} $$
The roots of $D_6$ are the permutations of $(\pm 1, \pm 1, 0, 0, 0, 0)$, and they generate $D_6$ rather than $\mathbb Z^6$. The 60 roots are the vertices of the **rectified 6-orthoplex**, which is therefore the *root polytope* of $D_6$. The Voronoi cell of $D_6$ is its polar dual: it has 60 facets (one per root) and 76 vertices, namely the 12 shallow holes $\pm e_i$ and the 64 deep holes $(\pm\tfrac12)^6$ (cf. $D_3$, whose Voronoi cell is the rhombic dodecahedron with $6+8=14$ vertices; Conway & Sloane, *Sphere Packings, Lattices and Groups*, 3rd ed., Ch. 4 and Ch. 21). The bare 6-orthoplex $\{\pm e_i\}$ is neither: its vertices are not $D_6$ vectors. The $F\cong D_6$ branch and its icosahedral projection remain a classified alternative to GCT's adopted primitive-$P$ branch.

The higher-symmetry $E_8$ relation is distinct: the McKay correspondence associates $2I$ with $\widetilde E_8$, and the Elser–Sloane construction projects the 240 $E_8$ roots onto two concentric $H_4$ 600-cell shells before the separate rank-six $H_3$ module is introduced. These results conditionally organize the architecture; they neither make $\mathbb Z^6$ a lattice subobject of even $E_8$ nor select the primitive Bravais type.

**CXL.2 The Projection Matrices (Explicit Forms)**

To generate the 3D Ammann-Kramer-Neri (AKN) tiling, we project the standard basis of $\mathbb{Z}^6$ into physical space $E_\parallel$ and internal space $E_\perp$. The two Galois-conjugate raw row frames below have different Euclidean norms: each parallel row has squared norm $2(2+\phi) \approx 7.236$ and each perpendicular row $2(3-\phi) \approx 2.764$. A single scalar cannot normalize both, so the canonical isometric convention uses two constants:
$$ N_\parallel = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2(2+\phi)}}, \qquad N_\perp = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2(3-\phi)}} = \phi N_\parallel . $$

**Parallel Projection Matrix ($\mathcal{M}_\parallel$):**
$$\mathcal{M}_\parallel = N_\parallel \begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 & 0 & 0 & \phi & \phi \\
\phi & -\phi & 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & \phi & -\phi & 1 & -1
\end{pmatrix}$$

**Perpendicular Projection Matrix ($\mathcal{M}_\perp$):**
Generated by the Galois conjugation $\phi \to -\phi^{-1}$:
$$\mathcal{M}_\perp = N_\perp \begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 & 0 & 0 & -\phi^{-1} & -\phi^{-1} \\
-\phi^{-1} & \phi^{-1} & 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & -\phi^{-1} & \phi^{-1} & 1 & -1
\end{pmatrix}$$

**Verification of Orthogonality [Tier 1 — algebraic identity]:**
The matrices satisfy $\mathcal{M}_\parallel \mathcal{M}_\perp^T = \mathbf{0}$. Computing the dot product of the first rows:
$$\mathbf{r}_{\parallel 1} \cdot \mathbf{r}_{\perp 1} \propto (1)(1) + (1)(1) + (0)(0) + (0)(0) + (\phi)(-\phi^{-1}) + (\phi)(-\phi^{-1})$$
$$= 1 + 1 + 0 + 0 - (\phi \cdot \phi^{-1}) - (\phi \cdot \phi^{-1}) = 2 - 1 - 1 = 0 \checkmark$$
By the $H_3$ icosahedral symmetry, all nine row-pair dot products vanish identically. With the two normalizations,
$$ \mathcal{M}_\parallel\mathcal{M}_\parallel^T = \mathcal{M}_\perp\mathcal{M}_\perp^T = I_3, \qquad \mathcal{M}_\parallel^T\mathcal{M}_\parallel + \mathcal{M}_\perp^T\mathcal{M}_\perp = I_6 . $$
This establishes kinematic orthogonality and completeness. Dynamical decoupling of phason ($E_\perp$) and phonon ($E_\parallel$) modes additionally depends on the quadratic action (App DPS §DPS.3). $\square$

*Coordinate-convention note:* a common scalar $N = 1/\sqrt{2(2+\phi)}$, used in earlier editions, makes only the parallel rows unit-normal; in that legacy chart $\mathcal{M}_\perp\mathcal{M}_\perp^T = \phi^{-2} I_3$, so it is not the orthonormal Euclidean projector. Rescaling the internal coordinates and the acceptance window together leaves the AKN model set unchanged. The two-normalization convention above is the projection source of truth for this appendix.

**CXL.3 The Star Map: Vertex-Configuration Count Conventions**

The discrete nature of the lattice restricts the local environment of any node to a finite set of "Vertex Stars." The phrase "vertex configuration" has, however, been used for three different objects, whose counts must not be interchanged:

- **24** — the historical count of a published matching-rule / vertex-configuration convention (Henley 1986 *Phys. Rev. B* 34:797; Katz & Duneau 1986 *J. Physique* 47:181). It has not been reproduced as the bare nearest-neighbour alphabet used by the current GCT engine and cannot be substituted for it.
- **9** — the bare edge-incidence stars of the RT-window model set under full $I_h$ equivalence: a bare star is the subset of the twelve allowed displacements $\{\pm e_i\}_{i=1}^{6}$ whose endpoints also lie in the window (App QLA §QLA.3) [Tier 1 enumeration conditional on the Tier 2 AKN projector/window].
- **101** — the radius-two decorated stars under the declared decoration convention (App QLA §QLA.3); this is a numerical cell-sampling result, not a completed formal arrangement proof or a universal AKN vertex count.

Earlier editions also cited a count of 32; that number is retired (it is not a vertex-star count in any of the three conventions; cf. App GRF §GRF.2.1).

Under the twelve-bond bare alphabet:
1. **The $\nu$-Star ($Z=12$):** the perfect twelve-bond icosahedral reference star is an allowed local configuration and a useful orientational control; it is not by itself a dynamically selected ground state.
2. **The conditional defect-cage ansatz ($N=144$):** this is not one vertex star but a proposed **cluster**: under the adopted Tier 3 O.38 architecture, 12 $\nu$-stars at the face centres of a dodecahedral shell (icosahedral positions) as a candidate electron defect. The identities $12^2=F_{12}=144$ and the exact polyhedral Gauss--Bonnet totals are internal consistency checks; they do not establish minimum energy, stability, or physical cage-size selection. Those claims remain open pending O.38 full-lattice minimization.
3. **The $\tau$ object ($Z=20$):** it cannot be a bare nearest-neighbour star in a twelve-displacement alphabet. If retained (as the cluster associated with the "Hole" modes of Volume 3), it must be typed as a larger-radius or decorated cluster whose graph and dynamics are specified separately [Tier 3/open].

The proposal that the elementary-particle spectrum can be read from the vibrational spectra of such defect cages is a Tier 3 research programme (O.38); no particle table has been derived from these geometric cages.

**CXL.4 Vacuum Supersolidity: Definition and Open Requirements [Tier 3/open]**

The condition for supersolidity (Andreev & Lifshitz, 1969) is that the ground state possesses both crystalline density order $\langle \rho(\mathbf{r}) \rangle = \sum_G \rho_G e^{i\mathbf{G}\cdot\mathbf{r}}$ and off-diagonal phase order $\langle \psi \rangle = \sqrt{\rho_0} e^{i\theta}$. This is a definition, not a derivation of the GCT vacuum state.
In a standard crystal, the atoms are localized (Peierls pinning), making phase transport impossible.
Quasiperiodicity and irrational projection do not by themselves imply zero-energy phason shifts, quantum tunneling, nonzero superfluid stiffness, or global phase coherence. The earlier "Phason-Assisted Tunneling" argument — that zero-energy phason shifts let nodes tunnel between configurations and so establish global phase coherence — is a Tier 3 hypothesis, not a derivation. Establishing it requires a normalized many-body Hamiltonian, ground state, transition amplitudes and thermodynamic limit. App ULA §ULA.2 provides a bosonic displacement scaffold with a symbolic $V_{lock}$; it does not yet establish off-diagonal long-range order or vacuum supersolidity. This is the same open status as V2 Ch03 Conditional Criterion 3.1.
