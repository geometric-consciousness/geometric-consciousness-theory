##### USS.7.2.2 The McKay correspondence (standard form)

The McKay correspondence (McKay 1980) is a bijection between conjugacy classes of finite subgroups of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and simply-laced affine Dynkin diagrams of type ADE. For the finite $SU(2)$ subgroup families:

| Binary group $\tilde{\Gamma}$ | Order | Base group $\Gamma \subset SO(3)$ | McKay graph | Lie algebra |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Binary cyclic $2C_n$ | $2n$ | $C_n$ | $\widetilde{A}_{2n-1}$ | $A_{2n-1}$ |
| Dicyclic $\mathrm{Dic}_n$ (binary dihedral) | $4n$ | $D_n$ (order $2n$) | $\widetilde{D}_{n+2}$ | $D_{n+2}$ |
| Binary tetrahedral $2T$ | 24 | $A_4$ | $\widetilde{E}_6$ | $E_6$ |
| Binary octahedral $2O$ | 48 | $S_4$ | $\widetilde{E}_7$ | $E_7$ |
| Binary icosahedral $2I$ | 120 | $A_5$ | $\widetilde{E}_8$ | $E_8$ |

The correspondence assigns to each $\tilde{\Gamma}$ the affine Dynkin diagram whose vertices are the irreducible complex representations of $\tilde{\Gamma}$ and whose edges are determined by the decomposition of the defining 2-dimensional representation tensored with each irrep (the "McKay multiplication rule"). The Lie algebra associated to $\tilde{\Gamma}$ is the finite ADE algebra obtained by deleting the affine node. [Tier 1 — closed in McKay 1980; modern textbook treatment in Steinberg, *Representation Theory of Finite Groups* §10.]

##### USS.7.2.3 The Elser–Sloane projection (standard form)

Elser and Sloane (1987, *J. Phys. A* 20: 6161–6168) construct an orthogonal decomposition $\mathbb R^8=X\oplus\widetilde X$ into two invariant four-dimensional spaces and project $E_8$ onto $X$. In their icosian coordinates, the 240 minimal vectors of $E_8$ are the 120 unit icosians together with $\sigma$ times those unit icosians, where $\sigma=(1-\sqrt5)/2=-\phi^{-1}$. Their images therefore form **two concentric 600-cell shells**, each with 120 vertices, whose radii differ by the factor $\phi$; they are not a doubly covered single shell. The coordinate-level source of truth is the orthogonal matrix $\Phi$ and projector $\Pi=\Phi\,\mathrm{diag}(I_4,0)\Phi^T$ in Elser–Sloane §2. ##### USS.7.2.4 The Atiyah–Patodi–Singer framework (standard form)

For a compact Riemannian manifold $M$ with boundary $\partial M$ and a Dirac operator $D$ acting on a spinor bundle, the Atiyah–Patodi–Singer index theorem (Atiyah, Patodi & Singer 1975, Math. Proc. Camb. Phil. Soc. 77: 43) states
$$\mathrm{ind}_{APS}(D) \;=\; \int_M \widehat{A}(M) \,\wedge\, \mathrm{ch}(\mathcal{E}) \;-\; \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(h(D|_{\partial M}) + \eta(D|_{\partial M})\bigr),$$
where $h$ is the dimension of the kernel of the boundary Dirac operator and $\eta$ is the Atiyah–Patodi–Singer $\eta$-invariant — the regularized spectral asymmetry of $D|_{\partial M}$. The boundary correction is the reduced invariant
$$\xi(D|_{\partial M}) \;=\; \tfrac12\bigl(h(D|_{\partial M})+\eta(D|_{\partial M})\bigr).$$
Along a smooth family of self-adjoint Dirac-type operators, eigenvalues crossing zero produce integer spectral-flow jumps. Passing to $\xi \bmod \mathbb{Z}$ removes those integer jumps, but does not by itself make the class constant: its smooth variation is governed by a local transgression term. Thus neither raw $\eta$ nor $\xi \bmod \mathbb{Z}$ supplies deformation invariance without an additional vanishing argument. The relevant finite-group rationality theorem is narrower: APS II, Theorem 2.9 proves rationality for the **relative** signature $\rho$-invariant attached to a flat unitary representation factoring through a finite covering group; the spin-Dirac analogue is naturally reduced modulo $\mathbb Z$. A raw $\eta$-value on a finite-symmetry orbit is not thereby rational. [Tier 1 — APS I, Theorem 3.10 for the boundary formula; APS II, §§2–3 for spectral-flow jumps and finite-group relative invariants.]

##### Lemma T-McK.1a (Spinor cover: $A_5 \Rightarrow 2I$) [Tier 1]

*Let $\Gamma = A_5 \subset SO(3)$ be the icosahedral rotation group. Then the unique universal central extension of $\Gamma$ by $\mathbb{Z}_2$ is the Binary Icosahedral Group $2I$, and $2I$ is the unique perfect double cover of $A_5$ inside $SU(2)$.*

*Proof.* The argument is closed by group cohomology. The Schur multiplier of $A_5$ is $H_2(A_5, \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}_2$ (Aschbacher, *Finite Group Theory*, 2nd ed., §33.15), and $A_5$ is perfect: $H_1(A_5, \mathbb{Z}) = A_5/[A_5, A_5] = 0$ (since $A_5$ is the smallest non-abelian simple group). A perfect group has a universal central extension, whose kernel is its Schur multiplier. For $A_5$, this extension is $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{F}_5) \cong 2I$, of order 120 (Conway et al., *ATLAS of Finite Groups*, p. 2; Steinberg, *Lectures on Chevalley Groups* §7). It is also the canonical inverse image of the embedded icosahedral rotation group $A_5\subset SO(3)$ under $SU(2)\to SO(3)$. $\square$

> **Remark.** This step is the algebraic content of the §USS.6.1 argument, restated as one closed correspondence within the conditional T-McKay architecture chain. The non-perfectness of $A_4$ (the tetrahedral case has $A_4/[A_4,A_4] = \mathbb{Z}_3 \neq 0$) and of $S_4$ (octahedral: $S_4/[S_4,S_4] = \mathbb{Z}_2 \neq 0$) makes their canonical double covers non-universal; it does not prevent the Spin$(3)$ preimages $2T$ and $2O$ from existing. ##### Lemma T-McK.2 (McKay-correspondence forcing: $2I \Rightarrow E_8$) [Tier 1]

*Under the McKay correspondence, the binary icosahedral group $2I \subset SU(2)$ is mapped to the simply-laced affine Dynkin diagram $\widetilde{E}_8$. Deleting the affine node yields the finite Dynkin diagram $E_8$, which is the unique simply-laced Lie algebra of rank 8 with a 240-vertex root system in $\mathbb{R}^8$.*

*Proof.* The McKay correspondence is a closed result (McKay 1980; modern proof in Slodowy, *Simple Singularities and Simple Algebraic Groups*, Springer LNM 815). The specific assignment $2I \to \widetilde{E}_8$ is verified by computing the McKay graph of $2I$: the 9 irreducible complex representations of $2I$ (of dimensions $1, 2, 2', 3, 3', 4, 4', 5, 6$) form the vertices, and the edges are computed from the decomposition of $V_2 \otimes V_i$ where $V_2$ is the defining 2-dimensional representation. The resulting graph is the affine Dynkin diagram $\widetilde{E}_8$ (Conway & Sloane, *Sphere Packings, Lattices, and Groups*, §4.10). Deletion of the affine node yields the $E_8$ Dynkin diagram, and the $E_8$ root system contains exactly 240 roots of squared length 2 — the largest exceptional simply-laced root system (Bourbaki, *Lie Groups and Lie Algebras*, Ch. VI §4). Among the exceptional McKay entries $2T, 2O, 2I$, $E_8$ is the unique one of rank $> 7$ (the binary dihedral family gives $D_{n+2}$ of unbounded rank). This proves the McKay assignment; it does not identify that root system with the physical parent lattice, and the rank-8 stage enters the architecture only through the adopted Elser–Sloane route. $\square$

The icosahedral Bravais classification distinguishes primitive $P\cong\mathbb Z^6$, face-centred $F\cong D_6$, and body-centred $I\cong D_6^*$ choices (Rokhsar, Mermin & Wright 1987). Thus the closed conclusion here is **rank 6**. ##### Lemma T-McK.4 (φ-slope forcing: the $H_4$ Cartan/root-metric slope) [Tier 1]

## USS.8 Theorem USS.8: Parity of the Axial SU(2) Holonomy [Tier 1 algebraic parity; Tier 3 physical selection premise]

**Statement:** For every integer weight $n$, the diagonal axial connection
$A_\theta = \frac{n}{2}\sigma_z$ has $2\pi$ holonomy
$$U_n=\exp(in\pi\sigma_z)=(-1)^n I_2.$$
Even $n$ gives the trivial holonomy $I_2$ and odd $n$ gives the order-2
central element $-I_2$. The holonomy therefore distinguishes only parity;
$2I$-equivariance does not algebraically select $n=1$ among the odd integers.

**Proof:**

*Step 1 — Stabilizer of the 5-fold axis.*
Let $\hat{a}_5$ be the directed 5-fold rotation axis of the icosahedron.
The stabilizer of $\hat{a}_5$ under the adjoint $SO(3)$ action of the
icosahedral group $I$ is:
$$\mathrm{Stab}_I(\hat{a}_5) = \mathbb{Z}_5$$
generated by a $72°$ rotation. Its SU(2) pre-image in the double cover
$2I$ is:
$$\mathrm{Stab}_{2I}(\hat{a}_5) = \mathbb{Z}_{10}$$
generated by $U_{\mathrm{gen}} = \exp(i\pi\sigma_z/5)$.
The cyclic-subgroup orders of $2I$ are exactly $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10\}$.

*Step 2 — Exact holonomy parity.*
For a diagonal axial connection $A_\theta = \frac{n}{2}\sigma_z$ with
$n\in\mathbb Z$:
$$\mathcal{P}\exp\!\left(i\int_0^{2\pi} A_\theta\, d\theta\right)
 = \cos(n\pi)I_2+i\sin(n\pi)\sigma_z=(-1)^nI_2.$$
Thus $U_n=I_2$ for even $n$ and $U_n=-I_2=U_{\mathrm{gen}}^5$ for odd $n$.
Both values lie in $\mathrm{Stab}_{2I}(\hat a_5)$, so the subgroup condition
supplies no finer distinction. $\square$

*Step 3 — Adopted physical selection premise [Tier 3 central-holonomy ansatz].*
The baryonic branch adopts a nontrivial-winding condition and chooses the
least positive integer weight satisfying it. Nontrivial holonomy excludes even
$n$; least-positive minimality then selects $n=1$ and hence
$$\boxed{A_\theta = \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma_z}.$$
This is an explicit physical premise, not an algebraic uniqueness
consequence of $2I$: the choice $A_\theta=\tfrac12\sigma_z$ is retained as a
simple Tier 3 central-holonomy ansatz whose loop holonomy is $-I_2$. A physical
bundle, loop, connection and adoption rule are still required to select it.

**Tier split:** The parity identity $U_n=(-1)^nI_2$ is Tier 1 algebra. The selection
of $n=1$ and the connection $A_\theta=\tfrac12\sigma_z$ are Tier 3 (central-holonomy
ansatz under the adopted least-positive/nontrivial-winding premise); an adopted
premise is not derived from the geometry. The $3\times5$ winding count of §USS.3
remains a Tier 2 structural counting mechanism. Deployment of the resulting $\phi^{-1}$ correction in the
