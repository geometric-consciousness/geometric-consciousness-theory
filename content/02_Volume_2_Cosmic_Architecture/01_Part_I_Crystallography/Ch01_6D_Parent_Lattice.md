**1.1.2 The Need for Higher Dimensions**

The parent space $\mathbb{R}^6$ in which the lattice is embedded possesses a strictly **Euclidean Signature** $(+,+,+,+,+,+)$. The distance between any two lattice points $\mathbf{n}$ and $\mathbf{m}$ is given by the standard Euclidean norm:
$$d^2 = \sum_{i=1}^6 (n_i - m_i)^2$$

**Theorem 1.1 (Euclidean Stability Ansatz for the Parent; formerly "Euclidean Necessity") [label kept for reference stability; Tier 2 — conditional architectural argument under the static-lattice ansatz, not a necessity result; cf. the conditional Proposition FTH.2, App FTH §FTH.2]:**
*Under the static-lattice ansatz, the embedding space of the fundamental parent lattice is taken to be Riemannian (Euclidean) rather than Lorentzian so that the vacuum ground state is bounded below.*

**Argument:**
Suppose the parent space possessed a Lorentzian signature (e.g., five spatial and one temporal dimension in 6D). The declared static-lattice ansatz takes the vacuum to be a static, minimum-energy configuration (the Hamiltonian constraint $\hat{H}\Psi = 0$ alone does not impose this). Under that ansatz, an indefinite quadratic deformation energy would not define the required bounded ground-state functional without additional constraints (displacements along the time-like axis would enter with the opposite sign, the standard route to **Tachyonic Instabilities**). The model therefore adopts a positive-definite parent metric. The emergence of a Lorentzian low-energy causal structure is treated separately in the acoustic-metric construction (Chapter 8). Volume 1 Chapter 12 supplies formal relational/ordinal-clock bookkeeping only; it does not derive a physical Selection Operator's ordinality or phenomenal time.

**1.1.3 The Unit Cell (The Planck Scale)**

The lattice constant of the parent space, $a_6$, defines the fundamental resolution of the Operating System. We define the 6D lattice spacing $a_6$ as the primitive length unit ($a_6 \equiv 1$ in lattice units). The SI value of $a_6$ (the manuscript convention is $a_6 = 2\ell_P$, App DPS §DPS.7) is obtained only after choosing ONE dimensional calibration anchor (see Appendix ULA.6).

The 6-dimensional unit cell is a hypercube with a volume $V_{cell} = a_6^6 = 64\,\ell_P^6$ under the convention $a_6 = 2\ell_P$ [Tier 3 — follows from the Planck-scale calibration of $a_6$ discussed heuristically in §1.1.4]. This hyper-volume represents the **Informational Pixel** of the universe. Within the GCT substrate, "Information" is defined by the **Topological Winding State** of each node. Each lattice site is modelled as a binary register (Presence/Absence) [Tier 3 modelling choice], so it carries at most one bit ($\ln 2$ nats) of structural information. This discreteness keeps the information content of any finite volume finite; because it scales with volume, it is not by itself compatible with the holographic area bound (§1.1.4).

**1.1.4 The Planck-Scale Calibration [Tier 3 heuristic; the Planck link inherits the Tier 4 conjecture O.14]**

Identifying the lattice constant with the Planck scale is a calibration choice, not a requirement. The heuristic motivation (Volume 1, Chapter 13, "Information Saturation") is that the Planck length is the scale at which the Compton and Schwarzschild lengths of a single localized excitation coincide up to a factor of order one; the "strain energy of a bit" in that picture is not defined by the present action.

Two considerations bound the choice only loosely. A lattice constant far above $\ell_P$ could produce dispersion observable at accessible energies; how strong that bound is depends on the order of the Lorentz-violating terms, which is itself a Tier 3 assumption (V2 Ch08 §8.3.5). The holographic bound does not select the Planck scale either: one bit per cell gives an information content that grows with volume, $\sim(L/a_6)^3$, and this exceeds the holographic area bound $\sim(L/\ell_P)^2$ once $L > a_6^3/\ell_P^2$, that is, for every macroscopic region when $a_6$ is of order $\ell_P$. Reconciling volume counting with an area law needs a separate argument (cf. the screen counting of Ch09 §9.1.6). The Planck scale is therefore adopted as the model's UV cutoff by calibration; it is not shown to be unique or forced.

**1.2.2 The Voronoi Decomposition**

To define the geometry of the "Atomic Surface" (The Acceptance Window), we must examine the **Voronoi Cell** of the $\mathbb{Z}^6$ lattice. A Voronoi cell is the region of space surrounding a node containing all points closer to that node than to any other. For a hypercubic lattice, the Voronoi cell is itself a **6D Hypercube**.

The complexity of our 3D physics arises not from the Voronoi cell's shape in 6D, which is simple, but from its **Projection**. When the 6D hypercubic unit cell is projected into the internal perpendicular space ($E_\perp$), it forms a **Rhombic Triacontahedron** (RT). As we shall see in Chapter 2, it is the icosahedral symmetry of this RT window that filters the 6D periodic data into the rich, aperiodic spectrum of matter and forces we observe.
