**13.1 The Fracture Transition**

**13.1.1 Yield Strength: The Virial Stress Limit [Tier 3 physical ansatz; Tier 2 conditional mechanics]**

Every solid medium is defined by its ability to support shear stress only up to a critical limit. In the GCT substrate, the **Topological Glass** consists of phason strain $w_{ij}$ pinned to the icosahedral matching rules of the vacuum. The energy required to maintain this strain is governed by the phason stiffness $K_\perp$.

We define the **Vacuum Yield Strength ($\sigma_c$)** as the maximum stress the phason field can support before the topological pinning of the lattice fails, and write it [Tier 3 ansatz] as a function of the critical strain $\epsilon_y$:
$$\sigma_c \approx \epsilon_y \cdot K_\perp$$
where the AKN tiling-flip proposal gives the candidate value $\epsilon_y = \phi^{-5} \approx 0.090$ in V3 Ch11 §11.1.3 **[Tier 3, conditional on two Tier 3 inputs]**. The calculations use $\epsilon_y = 0.1$ (Tier 3); $\phi^{-5}$ is a candidate value, not the operative one. Under the ansatz, the gravitational stress (virial stress) generated by baryonic mass in spiral galaxies would lie significantly below $\sigma_c$, leaving the vacuum in the elastic regime, while in rich galaxy clusters—where the total mass exceeds $10^{14}$ to $10^{15}$ solar masses—the accumulated gravitational potential would create a strain gradient exceeding the yield strength of the lattice. This conflicts with Protocol C's treatment of relaxed cool cores (Perseus, A2029, A1795) as below its frozen pressure cut; the cluster-core rheology is a declared open programme (V3 Ch11 §11.1.4), and neither reading is used to absorb or predict an observation.

**13.1.2 The Phase Change: Stress-Induced Liquefaction**

Under the Tier-3 transition ansatz, exceeding $\sigma_c$ labels a local phase-change branch. Conditional on that assumption, the tiles lose their pinned orientations and the model enters **Stress-Induced Liquefaction**; physical realization remains open.

* **Cluster Cores:** In the dense central regions of a cluster, the vacuum is in a **Fractured Liquid State** ($\eta_g \to 0$). In this regime, the shear modulus $\mu_\perp$ vanishes. Gravity ceases to mimic MONDian elasticity but instead follows the "Fluid" limit of General Relativity, where the "Dark Matter" is treated as a collisionless gas of energy density. Under the ansatz this would account for why cluster mass profiles require a dark component but do not obey the simple MOND acceleration law found in isolated galaxies [Tier 3; core rheology open, V3 Ch11 §11.1.4].
* **The Transition from Pinned to Unpinned:** In the glassy phase (Galaxies), the phason defects are "pinned" to the lattice, creating a static restoring force. In the fractured phase (Clusters), the defects become **Unpinned**. They become kinetic debris—independent topological singularities (vortons) that move freely through the superfluid background. Free emitters at cluster virial speeds would broaden the 3.55 keV line far beyond the narrow width assumed in Protocol C; the emitter kinematics are a declared open programme (V3 Ch11 §11.2.3).

**13.1.3 Resolution of the Bullet Cluster**

The "Bullet Cluster" (1E 0657-56) is often cited as strong evidence for particulate dark matter because its lensing centers are spatially separated from the baryonic gas. Conditional on the Chapter-12 and fracture ansätze, GCT offers **Rheological Separation** as a candidate postdiction; it neither refutes the particle interpretation nor derives the physical separation from first principles.

* **The Collision Event:** When two clusters collide, the immense stress shatters the pinned vacuum lattice.
* **Phase Separation:** The system separates into two phases with distinct viscosities:
 1. **The Phonon Sector (Baryons):** The gas interacts via the "stiff" phonon modes (electromagnetism/pressure), exhibiting high viscosity and shock fronts. It slows down.
 2. **The Phason Sector (Phason-Defect Halo):** The shattered vacuum energy forms a cloud of **Unpinned Phason Defects** ($E_\perp$). These defects are decoupled from the phonon metric; they interact only via the acoustic metric (Gravity).
* **The Result:** The proposed **phason-defect halo** moves ballistically, preserving its momentum like a collisionless fluid, and can therefore lead the viscous baryonic gas. This physical defect cloud is not the compact address Solenoid $\Sigma_\infty$. The observation is consistent with **Vacuum Superfluidity** [Tier 3] and does not uniquely favor a new particle, but it does not constitute a decisive confirmation. An inviscid (super)fluid is not automatically collisionless; that the defect gas behaves collisionlessly, and meets the Bullet-cluster self-interaction bound, is not shown.

**13.1.4 The Rheological Phase Diagram [Tier 2 conditional mechanics; Tier 3 physical adoption]**

We formalize this behavior by plotting the Vacuum Phase Diagram on the axes of **Shear Stress ($\tau$)** vs **Temperature ($T_{eff}$)** (where $T_{eff}$ represents the kinetic energy density of the defects).

1. **Solid Phase (Galaxies):** Below the critical stress ($\tau < \sigma_c$), the vacuum is a **Topological Glass**. The phason defects are pinned. The effective viscosity is infinite ($\eta_{visc} \to \infty$). This is the MONDian regime where the vacuum response is elastic.
2. **Fluid Phase (Clusters):** Above the critical stress ($\tau > \sigma_c$), the vacuum is a **Shear-Thinning Fluid**. The lattice yields, and the defects unpin. The viscosity drops precipitously ($\eta_{visc} \to 0$). This is the Dark Matter regime where the vacuum is assumed to flow as a collisionless gas (not shown; see §13.1.3).

**Mechanism of Separation:**
Within the assumed model, the Bullet Cluster impact region is assigned a stress spike $\tau \gg \sigma_c$, a superfluid-like vacuum branch, and viscous baryons governed by electromagnetic cross-sections. This rheological contrast is a candidate coordinate for the observed separation; its physical realization and causal sufficiency remain open.
